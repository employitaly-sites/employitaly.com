# Retirement ages and the long-tenure mindset in Italy

> Italian pension rules push careers toward long tenure. See what the retirement thresholds mean for how you plan hiring, retention and workforce age in Italy.

If you hire in Italy, you hire people who plan their careers around the pension system. The rules are not a detail in the background. They shape how long employees expect to stay, how they weigh a job change and what they expect from an employer over time.

This article explains the main thresholds and what they mean for a foreign company building a team in Italy.

## Two routes to retirement

Italy has two main routes to a pension. Both depend on years of contributions, and both move upward over time.

**Old-age pension.** The employee must reach a minimum age and have a minimum contribution record.

- In 2026: age 67 and 20 years of contributions.
- In 2027: age 67 and 1 month, with 20 years of contributions.
- In 2028: age 67 and 3 months, with 20 years of contributions.

**Early pension.** This route depends on contribution years alone, not on age. The requirement differs for men and women.

- In 2026: 42 years and 10 months for men, 41 years and 10 months for women.
- In 2027: 42 years and 11 months for men, 41 years and 11 months for women.
- In 2028: 43 years and 1 month for men, 42 years and 1 month for women.

The figures are published by the Italian press from official updates. They are secondary sources, so check them against the current rules before you plan around a specific case.

## What the numbers tell you

The direction is clear. Each year the bar rises. A person who starts work in Italy knows that the path to retirement is long and that continuity of contributions is what counts.

This has a practical effect on behaviour. Employees think in terms of a full career, not a series of short jobs. A gap in contributions delays retirement. A stable contract protects the record. So many Italian workers value steady employment more than a higher salary with a short horizon.

## The long-tenure mindset

Most foreign managers come from markets where changing employer every few years is normal. In Italy, many employees see it differently.

They tend to prefer:

- a permanent contract over a fixed-term one;
- an employer that looks stable and will still exist in the future;
- clear contribution payments, with no irregularities;
- predictable pay and benefits over variable pay.

This is not a lack of ambition. It is a rational reading of the pension system. When retirement is far away and depends on years of unbroken contributions, security has a high value.

## What it means for hiring

For an international company, this mindset can work in your favour.

**Retention can be strong.** If you offer a stable role and pay contributions correctly, many employees will stay. Turnover costs are lower when people want to build a long record with one employer.

**Experienced candidates are in the market.** Because the retirement age is rising, senior professionals stay in the workforce longer. You can hire people with deep experience and expect them to remain active for years.

**Offers must look credible.** A candidate who thinks in decades will ask about your presence in Italy. A foreign company with no local entity can look temporary. That is a real obstacle in recruiting, especially for senior roles.

**Exits need care.** Because careers are built over a long time, a dismissal affects the employee's whole path to retirement. Expect more resistance and more formality than in markets where employment is easy to end. Plan for this from the start.

## Planning your workforce

The pension thresholds also affect your own planning. Older employees may stay longer than you expect, so succession and knowledge transfer need attention. Younger employees may look for signs that the role leads somewhere over a long period.

It helps to be open about this in recruitment. Describe the contract type, the growth path and who the employer of record is. Candidates in Italy read these details closely.

## How an Employer of Record helps

An Employer of Record (EOR) hires the employee in Italy on your behalf. It issues a local contract, pays wages and contributions, and handles the pension and social security side. The employee gets the stability they look for. You direct the daily work without setting up a local company first.

This matters here because contribution accuracy is central to how Italian workers judge an employer. When payments are correct and on time, trust builds. When they are not, you lose it quickly.

## Summary

Retirement rules in Italy rise gradually, and both routes reward long, continuous contribution records. That shapes a workforce that values stability and plans for the long term. If you respect that, you can keep good people for a long time. If you ignore it, hiring will be harder than it needs to be.
