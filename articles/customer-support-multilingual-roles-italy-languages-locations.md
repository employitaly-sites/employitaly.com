# Hiring customer support and multilingual teams in Italy: language skills and locations

> How to hire customer support and multilingual staff in Italy: which language skills are available, where to base the team, and how to set it up without an entity.

Many international companies want a support team in Europe. Italy is a practical choice. The country has a large pool of graduates in languages, a strong university system and a culture that values service. This article explains how to think about language skills and location before you open a role.

## Start with the language map, not the job title

A "multilingual support agent" can mean very different things. Before you post a role, write down what the agent must actually do in each language.

- Answer written tickets and chats.
- Speak with customers on the phone.
- Handle escalations and complaints.
- Translate or review help center content.

Writing is easier to find than fast, natural speech. A candidate may read and write German well but hesitate on a live call. Test the exact skill you need. Do not rely on a CV line that says "fluent".

## Which languages are easy to find

Italian and English are the baseline. Most candidates in customer-facing roles can work in English. Check the depth, because a person who can follow a meeting may still struggle with a billing dispute.

Spanish, French and German are the most common additional languages. Many Italians study at least one of them at school or university. Portuguese, Russian and the Nordic languages are rarer. Arabic, Chinese and Japanese are also rarer, though you can find them in larger cities with international universities.

There is another source: native speakers who live in Italy. Expats, students, partners of people working in Italy, and Italians who grew up abroad often have a true native level in a second language. They are a strong fit for rare languages. They may need visa or permit support, which a provider can handle for you.

## Where to base the team

Location shapes who you can hire. There is no single best city. Match the city to the language mix.

**Milan.** The most international market. It has the widest mix of native speakers, many multinational employers and good transport links. Competition for talent is also highest here. Expect candidates to compare offers.

**Rome.** A large pool of graduates and many international organisations. Good for English, French and Spanish. It also has a sizeable community of foreign residents.

**Turin.** A solid base for French, thanks to its location and history. It has a strong technical and industrial background, which helps if your support involves technical products.

**Bologna and other university cities.** Large student populations mean a steady flow of young graduates with language degrees. Padua, Florence, Trieste and Naples also fit this pattern. Trieste has historic links with Central European languages.

**Remote across Italy.** Support work moves well to remote or hybrid models. You can hire from a wide range of cities and towns and avoid the cost and competition of the largest centres. Make sure candidates have a stable connection and a quiet workspace. Agree on equipment and data security rules from the start.

## Match shifts to the time zone

Italy sits in Central European time. This works well for customers in Europe, the Middle East and Africa. For North American coverage, you will need late shifts. For Asia-Pacific, you will need early ones. Italian employment rules cover working time, rest periods and night work. Plan your rota with these rules in mind, and do not copy a schedule from another country. Ask your provider to check any shift pattern before you promise it to candidates.

## Write the job ad for the real skill

Good ads are short and specific. State the languages, the channel (phone, chat or email) and the level of speaking you need. Say whether the role is on site, hybrid or remote. Mention the product and the type of customer. Candidates respond better to a clear description than to a long list of buzzwords.

Offer a language test as part of the process. A short call in the target language and a written task will tell you more than any certificate.

## Retention matters as much as hiring

Support roles can have high turnover everywhere. A few practices help in Italy.

- Give a clear contract and a clear career path, for example toward team lead or specialist roles.
- Pay fairly for language skills. Rare languages have more bargaining power.
- Respect local labour rules on leave, notice and working hours.
- Keep onboarding in Italian where it helps, and provide training materials in the language the person will work in.

People stay when the job is stable and the rules are clear.

## How to hire without a local entity

You do not need to set up a company in Italy to hire here. An Employer of Record (EOR) employs the person on your behalf. It handles the contract, payroll, social contributions and compliance. You direct the daily work. This is a good fit for a small support team that you want to test in a new market before you commit to a larger presence.

An EOR can also support relocation and work permits when you need a native speaker from outside the country. It acts as the employer in Italy, so your team has a local contract and local protections from day one.

## A simple plan

- List the languages and channels you need to cover.
- Choose the city or cities that match those languages.
- Define shifts that fit your customers' time zones and local rules.
- Write a specific ad and add a language test.
- Choose an employment route that lets you start without a local entity.

Start small, measure the results, and add languages as demand grows.
