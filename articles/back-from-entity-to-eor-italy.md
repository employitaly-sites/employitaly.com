# Can You Go Back from an Italian Entity to an EOR?

> Yes, you can move employees from your Italian entity back to an Employer of Record. Here are the common reversal scenarios and how the move works.

Yes. A company that set up an Italian entity can move its people back to an Employer of Record (EOR). It is less common than the other direction, but it happens, and it is workable if you plan it.

This article covers the reasons companies reverse course, what changes for employees, and what to settle before you start.

## Why companies go back to an EOR

The entity made sense when you expected steady growth. Then plans change. These are the usual triggers.

**Headcount falls.** A small team does not justify the fixed work of running a company. That work includes accounting, statutory filings, payroll and an administrator.

**The market is paused.** Leadership wants to keep a presence in Italy but cut fixed costs while it reassesses.

**A restructuring or acquisition.** The group is consolidating legal entities and wants fewer of them to maintain.

**The entity was premature.** You opened it before you had proof of demand. Now you want a lighter way to keep the team while you test.

**Management load.** Nobody in the group wants to own Italian compliance any more.

## What the move actually is

In plain terms, the employer changes. Today your entity is the employer. After the move, the EOR is. The employees do the same work for the same business, but their contract sits with a different legal employer.

This is a transfer of employment, not a layoff followed by a rehire. That distinction matters. It affects seniority, accrued rights and how employees see the change.

## What employees care about

Employees will ask the same questions in every case.

- Will my length of service be recognised?
- Will my pay and benefits stay the same?
- Will my accrued leave and end-of-service entitlements be protected?
- Who do I contact for payroll and HR questions?

You should have clear written answers before you announce anything. Italian employment rules give workers real protections. A poorly handled transfer can create disputes that cost far more than the entity ever did. Treat consent and communication as part of the project, not as paperwork at the end.

## Steps to plan

**1. Decide the scope.** Are you moving everyone or only some roles? Will the entity close, or stay as a dormant shell?

**2. Map every contract.** List each employee, their collective agreement, role level, pay, benefits and any individual agreements. The EOR needs this to replicate terms accurately.

**3. Settle accrued entitlements.** Decide with your advisors whether accrued items such as leave and end-of-service pay are paid out or carried over. Both can work. The choice must be documented and agreed.

**4. Agree the transfer in writing.** Each employee should understand and accept the new employer arrangement. Your Italian labour counsel should review the documents.

**5. Plan the payroll cutover.** Pick a clean switch point at a pay period boundary. Avoid overlap, where two employers pay the same person, and avoid gaps.

**6. Handle registrations.** Social security and tax registrations tied to the entity as employer need to be closed or updated. The EOR registers the employees under its own accounts.

**7. Deal with the entity itself.** Do not start winding it down until every employee has moved and the final payroll filings are done. Contracts, premises, bank accounts and data all need an owner during the transition.

## Risks to watch

**Continuity gaps.** If service is not clearly recognised, employees can claim they lost rights.

**Your role in the work.** The EOR is the legal employer. You still direct the daily work. Keep that line clear, and do not let your group set contract terms outside the EOR agreement.

**Timing with the entity's closure.** Closing too early leaves filings unfinished. Closing too late keeps costs running.

**Tax exposure.** If employees keep doing senior or deal-making work, your group may still carry tax questions about its presence in Italy. Get advice before you decide the entity can go.

## When it is not the right move

If you plan to hire many people over the long term, the entity may still be the better structure. Going back to an EOR fits best when your Italian team is small, uncertain or temporary. It also fits when you want to hand off compliance work and keep the people.

If you expect to grow again later, the path stays open. Many companies start with an EOR, open an entity, and some later return to an EOR. The structure should follow your business needs, not the other way round.

## Practical advice

Start with a full contract review, not a price comparison. Know your employees' terms before you speak to an EOR. A good provider will ask for those details first, because accurate terms are the base of a clean transfer.

Talk to employees early and plainly. Most worries come from silence.
