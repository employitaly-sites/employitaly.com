# How to Write a Job Ad That Works in Italy

> A practical guide for foreign employers: the tone, structure and must-have details of a job ad that attracts good candidates in Italy.

A job ad written for your home market often falls flat in Italy. The words may be right. The signals are not. Italian candidates read ads with a few clear questions in mind: What is the role? What is the contract? What is the pay? Who will I work for?

This guide covers tone, structure and the details you should always include.

## Write in Italian

If the role is based in Italy, publish the ad in Italian. An English-only ad narrows your pool and signals that the job is not really rooted in the country. Candidates may read English well, but they notice when a company has not made the effort.

If the role needs English, say so in the Italian text and state the level you expect. You can add a short English version below it. Keep the Italian as the main text.

Do not machine-translate and post. Have a native speaker read it. Stiff or odd phrasing reads as careless.

## Get the tone right

Italian professional writing leans more formal than many English-speaking markets. Aim for clear and respectful, not stiff.

- Use plain, direct sentences.
- Address the reader in a polite, professional way.
- Avoid slang, jokes and internal jargon.
- Skip hype. Words like "rockstar" or "ninja" put people off.
- Do not promise a "family" culture. Describe the work instead.

Be warm, but be specific. Candidates trust concrete detail more than enthusiasm.

## Use a simple structure

A good ad is easy to scan. Use this order.

1. **Job title.** Use a standard title that people search for. Avoid invented titles. If you use an English title, add the Italian equivalent.
2. **Who you are.** Two or three sentences on the company, what it does and where it operates. Say clearly that you are a foreign company hiring in Italy, and explain how the hire will be employed.
3. **The role.** Describe the main tasks and what success looks like. Keep it to a short list.
4. **Requirements.** Split into must-have and nice-to-have. Long wish lists discourage strong candidates.
5. **What you offer.** Contract type, pay, working model, benefits.
6. **How to apply.** One clear step, with a named contact or a simple form.

Keep the whole ad short enough to read in a couple of minutes.

## Include the must-haves

These are the details Italian candidates look for. If they are missing, many will move on.

**Contract type.** Say whether the role is permanent or fixed-term, and whether it is full-time or part-time. Italian candidates care a great deal about contract stability. Vague wording raises suspicion.

**Pay.** State a salary range. Ads without pay information get less trust and fewer applications. Say whether the figure is gross annual pay. Be consistent with how the market quotes salary.

**Collective agreement.** Italian employment is shaped by national collective agreements. Naming the agreement that applies tells candidates you understand local rules. It also shows what they can expect on leave, notice and working time.

**Employer identity.** Candidates want to know who their legal employer is. If you use an Employer of Record (EOR), say so in plain words. Explain that the EOR is the formal employer in Italy and that day-to-day work is directed by your company. This avoids confusion at the offer stage.

**Work location and model.** State the city or region. Say if the role is on-site, hybrid or remote. If remote, say which areas of Italy are fine.

**Benefits.** List what is real. Training, equipment, flexible hours and health cover all matter. Only list what you will actually provide.

**Language requirements.** Name the languages needed for the job and the level.

## Be fair and inclusive

Write the ad so that it does not exclude anyone without a job-related reason. Do not ask for a photo, age, marital status or family plans. Use neutral wording for the job title and the text. Where the Italian title has gendered forms, show both or use a neutral form.

## Avoid common mistakes

- Posting a translated copy of a US or UK ad without adapting it.
- Hiding pay until the third interview.
- Listing ten requirements when two are essential.
- Using a title that no one in Italy searches for.
- Leaving out the employer's legal identity.
- Asking for a long application form up front.

## Where to post

Post where Italian candidates actually look. Use the major professional networks and the main Italian job boards. Add niche boards for specialist roles. Share the ad with your own network too, since personal referrals carry weight in Italy.

## Reply fast

Speed shows respect. Confirm receipt of each application. Give a clear timeline. Tell unsuccessful candidates. A reputation for good manners spreads quickly in a professional community.

## Final check

Before you publish, ask four questions.

- Could a candidate tell the contract, pay and location in under a minute?
- Is the Italian natural to a native reader?
- Is the employer's legal identity clear?
- Is every claim true?

If the answer to all four is yes, the ad is ready.
