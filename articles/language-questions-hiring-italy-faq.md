# Language Questions When Hiring in Italy: Contract Language, Workplace English and Documents

> Answers to common language questions when hiring in Italy: which language the contract uses, whether your team can work in English, and how documents are handled.

Foreign companies hiring in Italy often ask the same language questions. Do we need an Italian contract? Can the team work in English? Who handles the paperwork? This FAQ gives direct answers.

## Does the employment contract have to be in Italian?

In practice, yes. Italian employment relationships sit inside an Italian legal framework. The contract, the hiring notices and the payroll records are normally prepared in Italian.

Many employers also give the employee an English version. This is common and helpful. The employee understands what they sign, and your headquarters can read it too.

The key point is which version prevails if the two differ. Decide this in advance. The usual choice is that the Italian text controls and the English text is a courtesy translation. Ask your Employer of Record (EOR) to confirm how they handle this before the offer goes out.

## Can we hire someone who does not speak Italian?

Yes. Many international companies hire English-speaking staff in Italy, especially in technology, sales and management roles. The employee does not need to speak Italian to be employed.

The employee will still receive some documents in Italian. Examples are payslips, tax and social security forms and official letters. Your EOR can explain these in English. Make sure this is part of the service before you sign.

## Can the team work in English day to day?

Yes. You choose the working language of your team. Meetings, tools, code, reports and internal chat can all be in English. Italian law does not force a company to run its daily work in Italian.

Some areas are different:

- Written policies that the employee must understand and follow should be provided in a language they can actually read.
- Safety instructions and training must be clear to the worker. If someone does not read Italian well, give them materials they can understand.
- Formal notices about the employment relationship, such as disciplinary matters, should go through your EOR and follow Italian form.

A simple rule works well. Daily work can be in English. Anything with legal weight should be in Italian, with an English copy for the employee.

## Which collective agreement language applies?

Most Italian employment is shaped by a national collective agreement for the sector. These agreements are published in Italian. They set rules on pay levels, working hours, leave and notice.

You do not need to read them in full. Your EOR should tell you which agreement applies and what it means for your role in plain English. Ask for a short summary, not just the document.

## What about payslips and payroll documents?

Payslips are issued in Italian. Employees who do not read Italian often ask for help the first time they see one. A good EOR provides a short guide that explains each line in English. Ask for it at onboarding.

## Do foreign documents need translation?

Often they do. If a new hire brings diplomas, certificates or records from another country, Italian authorities may ask for an Italian translation. In some cases the translation must be certified.

This matters most for work visas and residence procedures. The authorities work in Italian. Application forms, supporting papers and appointments are handled in Italian. Your EOR or relocation partner should prepare and check these documents so the candidate does not face the process alone.

If you are bringing talent from abroad, ask early which papers need translation. It is better to find out before the start date than after.

## Who talks to the Italian authorities for us?

Your EOR does. They register the employment, run payroll and deal with social security bodies in Italian. You get updates in English. This is one of the main reasons companies use an EOR instead of opening a local entity.

## Can we send our own templates and policies?

Yes, as inputs. Share your code of conduct, IT policy or handbook in English. Your EOR can tell you what can be used as is and what needs to be adapted or translated. Do not assume a global template will fit Italy without a check.

## Quick checklist before you hire

- Agree which contract version prevails.
- Ask for an English copy of the contract.
- Ask for an English guide to payslips.
- Confirm that safety and policy materials are understandable to the employee.
- Find out which foreign documents need translation.
- Confirm that all contact with Italian authorities goes through your EOR.

Language is rarely a blocker. With the right setup, your team works in English and the Italian paperwork stays out of sight.
