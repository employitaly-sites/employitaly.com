# Job grades and levels in Italian collective agreements

> How inquadramento works in Italy: levels in the national collective agreement set your minimum pay, your job title and the duties you can assign.

If you hire in Italy, you will meet the word *inquadramento*. It means the level an employee is placed in under the national collective agreement (CCNL) that applies to your sector. This level matters more than most foreign employers expect.

## What a level is

Each CCNL contains a classification system. It groups jobs into broad categories, such as blue-collar workers, white-collar employees, middle managers and executives. Inside the categories, the agreement lists levels. Each level has a written description of the tasks, the autonomy and the skills it covers.

The agreement also lists example roles for each level. These examples are a guide. They are not a closed list.

## Levels set the salary floor

Every level in the CCNL has a minimum monthly pay. Italians call it the *minimo tabellare*. You cannot pay below it. The minimum rises as the level rises.

The agreement may add other fixed items on top of the base pay. These can include seniority steps, allowances and extra monthly payments. The mix differs between sectors. So the same job can have a different cost floor in retail, in metalworking and in IT services.

You can pay above the floor. Many employers do, especially for scarce skills. The floor is a legal minimum. It is not a market rate.

## Duties decide the level, not the title

This is the point that surprises most international teams. In Italy, the level follows the work the person actually does. It does not follow the title on the contract.

A company may call someone "Head of Growth" or "Senior Associate". Italian law and the CCNL look at the real tasks. If the person does work that fits a higher level, they can claim that level. They can also claim the pay difference for the period they worked at that higher level. Courts have ruled on this many times.

The reverse also holds. A grand title does not force you to pay a higher level if the duties do not match.

## How titles and levels fit together

You can use any job title you like. A title is a label for your org chart, your clients and LinkedIn. The level is a legal and payroll fact.

A clean approach is to keep both in the contract. The title says what the role is called. The level says how it is classified under the CCNL. Payroll, social contributions and some rights follow the level.

Keep your global grading system if you have one. Just map each of your grades to a CCNL level before you make an offer. Do this per role, not per country.

## How to choose the right level

Start from the job, not from the salary you want to offer.

1. Choose the correct CCNL for the sector and the activity of the employer.
2. Write a short list of the real daily tasks.
3. Compare those tasks with the level descriptions in the agreement.
4. Check the example roles for the closest match.
5. Set the pay at or above the minimum for that level.

If a role sits between two levels, look at autonomy and responsibility. Does the person decide alone, or do they follow set procedures? Do they manage others? Do they sign off on budgets? These factors often tip the choice.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the wrong CCNL.** The level table and the pay floors change with the agreement. A wrong choice breaks everything that follows.
- **Copying a foreign grade.** A "Senior" in your home country may not match a senior level in Italy.
- **Setting the title first.** A title promised in a job offer can create expectations about level and pay.
- **Ignoring role growth.** People take on more tasks over time. If the real work moves up, the level should follow.
- **Fixing the level only once.** Review it when duties change. Do it before a dispute starts.

## Why it matters for budgeting

The level drives your base cost. It also affects notice periods, sick pay rules and other terms that the CCNL links to classification. A one-level error on a team of several people adds up fast.

For a company planning its first Italian hire, a level check should come before the offer letter. It is cheap to do. It is expensive to correct later.
