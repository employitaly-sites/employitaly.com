# How to Close a Candidate Who Has a Competing Italian Offer

> A candidate in Italy holds another local offer. Here is how to win them on trust, contract clarity, career and speed, without raising the salary.

You found a strong candidate in Italy. Then they tell you they have another offer from an Italian company. Your first instinct may be to raise the salary. Often that is the wrong move, and sometimes it is not even possible.

This article covers what else you can offer, and how to present it.

## Understand what the candidate fears

A candidate with a local offer compares you to a known quantity. The local company has an office, a legal entity and a name they can look up. You are a foreign company they may never have heard of.

So the real question is rarely money. It is risk. Ask directly:

- What worries you about joining a foreign company?
- What does the other offer give you that you value most?
- What would make you say yes today?

Listen for words like stability, contract, growth, team, flexibility. Those are your levers.

## Remove the employer risk

Many Italian candidates worry that a foreign company will not treat them like a normal local employee. They fear payment delays, missing contributions, or a contract that is hard to enforce.

An Employer of Record (EOR) answers this fear. The candidate signs an Italian employment contract with a local employer. They get Italian payslips, the right social security registration and a collective agreement that applies to their role. Their rights are the same as if they worked for an Italian company.

Say this plainly. Do not wait for the candidate to ask. Explain who the legal employer is, who pays them, and who they call if something goes wrong in payroll.

## Show the contract early

Uncertainty kills offers. A competing company may have already sent a full contract draft. If you only send an email with a salary figure, you look less serious.

Share a clear summary of the terms as soon as you can:

- Type of contract and role title
- Applicable collective agreement
- Working hours and remote work rules
- Paid leave and other benefits
- Probation terms and notice terms

Let the candidate read it, ask questions and take it to a trusted advisor. Openness builds trust faster than any pitch.

## Sell the work, not only the package

A salary is a number. A job is what the person does every day. Candidates in Italy often join a small local company where the scope is narrow and the path upward is slow. You may offer something different.

Think about what is true for your company and say it:

- Exposure to international customers and teams
- Real ownership of a product, market or project
- A clear path to a larger role as the Italian presence grows
- Working in English with colleagues in several countries
- Direct access to leaders who make decisions

Be specific. Name the project they would start with. Name the person they would report to. Offer a call with that manager so the candidate meets the real team, not just a recruiter.

## Offer flexibility that costs you little

Many local employers still expect people in the office on most days. If your team is built for remote or hybrid work, that is a real advantage. State it in the contract, not just in conversation.

Other low cost items can matter more than a small salary increase:

- Flexible hours that fit family life
- A budget for training and conferences
- Equipment of the candidate's choice
- Support for a home office

These show respect for how the person wants to work.

## Handle the notice period with care

If the candidate is currently employed, they will have to give notice. A local competitor may offer a faster start. Do not push the candidate to leave badly. Italian professional circles are small, and a candidate who burns a bridge may carry that stress into your company.

Instead, agree a realistic start date. Stay in contact during the notice period. A short message from the future manager, an invitation to a team call or an early welcome pack keeps you present while the other company waits.

## Move fast, but stay calm

Speed matters. If the other company decides in days and you take weeks, you lose. Prepare your internal approvals before you make the offer. Know who signs, what the limits are and how quickly the EOR can issue the contract.

But do not use pressure tactics. Short fake deadlines make a candidate suspicious. Give a fair time to decide and be available for questions.

## If you must discuss money

Sometimes the salary gap is real. You can still stay flexible without simply raising the base pay.

- Look at the structure of the package, not only the total
- Consider a bonus linked to clear goals
- Consider a review of the role level after a set period
- Explain total employment cost and what the candidate receives, so the comparison is honest

An honest comparison often helps you. Candidates see the whole picture and judge on facts.

## Know when to let go

Some candidates will take the local offer. That is fine. Thank them, keep the relationship warm and ask for feedback. The reason they give tells you what to fix in your next process. Often the answer is not salary at all.

## Summary

When a candidate holds a competing Italian offer, work on trust first. Explain the local employer, show the contract, describe the real job and offer flexibility. Move quickly and treat the candidate with respect. Salary matters, but it is rarely the only reason someone says yes.
