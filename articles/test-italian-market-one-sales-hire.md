# How to Test the Italian Market With One Sales Hire

> Design a low-risk market test for Italy: hire one salesperson, set clear goals, limit fixed costs and decide in advance when to scale or stop.

Entering a new country is a bet. You can make it smaller. One sales hire, set up with a clear test design, tells you most of what you need to know about Italy before you commit to an entity, an office or a team.

This article explains how to design that test so the downside stays small.

## Start with the question, not the hire

A market test answers a question. Write it down before you recruit anyone.

Good questions are specific:

- Do Italian buyers respond to our offer in Italian?
- Can we win deals with our current price and contract terms?
- Which type of customer converts fastest?
- Does our product need local proof, such as references or certifications?

Bad questions are vague: "Can we succeed in Italy?" You cannot answer that with one person in a short time. You can answer the four above.

Pick one or two questions. Everything else in the design follows from them.

## Choose the right profile

A test hire is not a country manager. Do not hire someone who needs a team to be effective.

Look for a salesperson who:

- has sold similar products to similar buyers in Italy
- can open doors with their own network
- is comfortable working without a local office
- can write and speak well in Italian and in your company language
- likes building process, not only closing deals

Seniority matters less than fit. A strong individual contributor with a relevant network will often produce more signal than an executive who expects support staff.

## Keep fixed costs low

The purpose of the test is to limit what you lose if the answer is no. So limit commitments that are hard to reverse.

Avoid, for now:

- renting an office
- setting up a local company before you know you need one
- hiring support roles
- buying local tools and licences you may not use

A remote salesperson with a laptop, a phone and access to your CRM is enough. If you need a legal and payroll structure to employ them, use a model that lets you hire without building your own entity first. An Employer of Record (EOR) does this. It employs the person on your behalf in Italy, handles payroll, contributions and compliance, and you direct their daily work.

This keeps your setup time short and your exit simple.

## Define success before you start

Write the targets and the stop rules at the beginning. Do it together with the hire, so both sides understand the test.

Use leading indicators as well as revenue. Sales cycles are often longer than a short test period, so revenue alone can mislead you.

Track:

- number of qualified conversations
- number of demos or meetings held
- number of proposals sent
- reasons for lost deals
- feedback on price, language and product fit

Then set three decisions in advance:

1. **Scale:** what results make you add people or open an entity.
2. **Adjust:** what results mean you change the offer, price or target segment.
3. **Stop:** what results mean you end the test.

If you decide these after the fact, you will argue yourself into whatever you hoped to see.

## Give the hire what they need to sell

Many tests fail because the company sends a person to Italy with nothing local to sell with.

Prepare before day one:

- Italian-language website pages, a deck and a one-page offer
- pricing in euros and terms that Italian buyers can sign
- a clear process for contracts, invoicing and support
- a named person at headquarters who answers within a day
- a lead list or ideal customer profile to start from

If these are missing, you are testing your own preparation, not the market.

## Plan the employment side early

The employment contract is part of the test design. Italian employment is governed by national collective agreements that set parts of pay, benefits and notice rules. Your hire's package should follow the agreement that fits the role. An EOR will tell you which one applies and what the full cost looks like, so you can build the test budget on real numbers and not guesses.

Also decide early how you will handle:

- the structure of pay, including any variable part tied to results
- expenses and travel
- what happens at the end of the test, whether you continue, convert to your own entity, or part ways

Ask about the exit terms before you sign. A low-risk test needs a low-friction exit.

## Review on a fixed rhythm

Hold a short review at regular intervals. Look at the indicators, the lost-deal reasons and the customer feedback. Keep a written log.

At the end of the test, answer the questions you started with. Be honest about what the data shows and what it does not.

If the result is positive, you now have a person, a pipeline and real knowledge of the market. If it is negative, you have lost little and you know why.

## What you get from this approach

One hire will not tell you everything. It will tell you whether Italian buyers want what you sell, at your price, in your way. That is the information you need before a larger investment.

Small scope, clear questions, written stop rules and an easy exit. That is the whole design.
