# Entity readiness checklist for Italy: what to have in place before you set up

> A plain checklist for foreign companies planning to hire in Italy: decisions, documents and people to line up before you start a local entity or use an EOR.

Hiring in Italy starts well before the first contract. Companies that prepare the basics move faster and avoid rework. This checklist covers what to settle before you set up a local entity, or before you choose an Employer of Record (EOR) instead.

## Decide the route first

You have two main paths. You can open your own Italian entity. Or you can use an Employer of Record (EOR), which employs the person on your behalf while you direct the daily work.

Ask these questions before you pick:

- Do you plan to hire many people in Italy, or only a few?
- Do you need a local presence for customers, contracts or regulators?
- How soon does the first hire need to start?
- Are you testing the market, or committing to it?

A short test with a small team often fits an EOR. A long-term local operation often fits an entity. Write the answer down. It shapes everything below.

## Corporate documents

Whether you open an entity or not, your parent company needs clean records. Gather these early:

- Certificate of incorporation or equivalent registry extract
- Articles of association or bylaws
- Proof of the registered address
- A list of directors and authorised signatories
- Proof of who owns the company

Italian authorities and banks often ask for official translations and legalisation of foreign documents. Ask your advisor which documents need an apostille or a sworn translation. Start this early. It is slow and it blocks other steps.

## People and signatories

Someone must be able to sign for the company in Italy. Decide who:

- Who will be the legal representative of the Italian entity?
- Who approves hiring decisions?
- Who owns the relationship with the Italian payroll and tax providers?

If the person is not in Italy, check what identification and tax codes they need to act locally. Foreign directors usually need an Italian tax code before they can sign or open accounts.

## Banking and funding

An Italian entity needs a local bank account. Opening one can take time, and banks ask many questions about the parent group and its owners. Prepare:

- A clear description of what the entity will do
- Ownership and beneficial owner details
- A plan for how you will fund payroll and running costs

Decide how salaries will be funded each month. Late funding delays pay, and late pay creates problems with employees and with authorities.

## Registrations and compliance setup

An employer in Italy must be registered with the right bodies for tax, social security and workplace insurance. You also need a payroll process, a way to issue payslips, and a way to file the required reports on time.

Before you start, confirm:

- Who will run payroll and who will check it
- Who will handle social security and tax filings
- Who will keep employee records in the required form
- Who will handle workplace health and safety duties

With an EOR, these duties sit with the EOR. You still need to supply accurate data on time.

## Know your collective agreement

Italy uses national collective agreements by sector. They set minimum pay levels, working hours, leave, notice and other terms. The agreement that applies depends on your business activity and the role.

Before you write an offer, identify the agreement that fits. Do not copy the terms from another country. Check the job level and the pay against that agreement. This single step prevents many disputes later.

## Prepare the hiring package

Have these ready before you approach candidates:

- A clear job description and reporting line
- A salary range checked against the applicable agreement
- A start date that leaves time for registrations
- A draft of the employment terms, reviewed locally
- A plan for equipment, software access and data protection

The employment contract in Italy should be in line with local rules and often in Italian. Plan for that.

## If the hire is not an EU citizen

If the person needs permission to live and work in Italy, treat this as a separate project. It has its own documents, its own timeline and its own risks. Do not promise a start date until the route is confirmed. Check whether the person already has the right to work, and what type of permit would apply.

## Data protection and policies

You will process personal data of Italian employees. Prepare a privacy notice, a basic data processing record and a plan for how HR data is stored and shared with the parent company. Add internal policies that employees must receive, such as IT use and health and safety information.

## Quick checklist

- Route chosen: own entity or EOR
- Parent company documents gathered and translated
- Legal representative and signatories named
- Bank and funding plan ready
- Payroll and filing owners assigned
- Collective agreement identified
- Job description, pay and start date agreed
- Work permit route checked, if needed
- Data protection basics prepared

If most of these are done, you are ready to move. If several are missing, fix them before you commit to a start date.
