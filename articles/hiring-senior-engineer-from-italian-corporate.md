# Hiring a Senior Engineer Away From an Italian Corporate

> How to hire a senior engineer from an Italian corporate: handle the counter-offer, plan for notice, and set clear expectations from the first conversation.

You have found a senior engineer who works at a large Italian corporate. They are good, they are interested, and they want to talk. This is the point where many foreign companies lose the candidate. Not because the offer is weak, but because they misread how the exit works in Italy.

This article covers three things: the counter-offer, the notice period, and what each side expects.

## Expect a counter-offer

Large Italian employers rarely let a senior engineer leave without a response. When the resignation lands, the manager will often ask for time. HR may come back with a raise, a new title, or a promise of a project the engineer has wanted for years.

Plan for this before you make your offer. Do not treat it as a possibility. Treat it as the default.

Three things help.

First, find out why the person is leaving. If it is money, a counter-offer can win. If it is scope, ownership, remote work, or a ceiling on growth, money rarely fixes it. Ask directly in the interview. Ask again before you send the offer.

Second, ask the candidate how they will respond. A candidate who has thought about it will tell you plainly. A candidate who says "they would never do that" has not thought about it.

Third, make your offer about the whole role, not only the salary. Say what they will own. Say who they will report to. Say what you will build in the first year. A counter-offer can match a number. It is much harder for it to match a clear future.

## Do not compete on salary alone

Corporate packages in Italy often include more than base pay. There may be variable pay, a company car, supplementary health cover, meal benefits, and a long record of seniority. The engineer will compare your offer with all of it.

Put your offer in the same terms. Show the full annual cost and the full value to them, line by line. If your package is lower on one item, say so and explain what is higher elsewhere. Candidates trust a clear comparison more than a vague claim of "competitive pay".

## Notice is real and it is long

In Italy, notice is set by the employment contract and the applicable national collective agreement. For senior staff it is usually longer than what many foreign hiring managers are used to. It often depends on seniority and job level.

This has practical effects.

- Your start date will be later than you want. Ask for the real notice terms before you set any project plan.
- The candidate may offer to negotiate an earlier exit. Some employers agree, many do not. Do not push the candidate to burn bridges for your timeline.
- Notice is also the window when the counter-offer happens. A long notice gives the current employer time to work on the person.

Stay in contact during this period. Keep it light and useful. Share the team plan, introduce a future colleague, send the onboarding outline. The goal is to keep the engineer connected to the new job while the old one is still pulling.

If you have no Italian entity, remember that the engineer cannot start working for you until the employment relationship is in place. An Employer of Record can issue the contract and handle payroll and social contributions from the first day, so the start date is limited only by the notice, not by your setup time.

## Check restrictive clauses early

Senior engineers sometimes have confidentiality terms, non-compete terms, or clauses on client contact. These vary by contract. Ask the candidate to read their own agreement and tell you what applies. Do not ask them to share confidential material. If a clause looks relevant, get local advice before you finalise the offer.

A clean exit protects the engineer and protects you.

## Set expectations on both sides

What the engineer expects from you:

- A written offer that is clear and complete.
- A proper Italian employment contract under the right collective agreement, not a vague contractor arrangement.
- Payslips, leave, and benefits that look normal for the role.
- A manager who understands that Italian working practices differ from your home market.
- A real answer on remote work, travel, and working hours.

What you can expect from the engineer:

- Professional behaviour towards the current employer during notice.
- Honest communication if the counter-offer is tempting.
- A strong sense of duty to finish handover well. In a corporate setting this is often a matter of personal pride.
- Questions about stability. A corporate employee leaving a large, known name is taking a visible risk. They will want to see that your company will still be there.

That last point matters. Be ready to explain your funding, your customers, and your plans in the market. Do not oversell. Senior people notice.

## A simple sequence that works

1. Agree the real reason for leaving.
2. Make a full, written offer that covers role, pay, benefits, and growth.
3. Ask how the candidate will handle a counter-offer.
4. Confirm notice terms and any restrictive clauses.
5. Prepare the contract so it can be signed as soon as the candidate is free to commit.
6. Keep steady, low-pressure contact through the notice period.
7. Plan onboarding for the first day, not the first month.

## The takeaway

Hiring a senior engineer from an Italian corporate is a slow, human process. The counter-offer is likely, the notice is long, and the engineer is weighing stability against opportunity. Be clear, be patient, and show a future that a raise cannot copy.
