# How to Hire Your First Italian Team Lead: Profile and Scope

> Hiring a team lead in Italy for the first time? Define the profile, the scope and the reporting line before you write the job ad.

Your first Italian team lead sets the tone for everything that follows. They will hire the next people. They will explain your company to the local team. They will tell you what works in Italy and what does not.

So the profile and the scope matter more than the salary discussion. Get these right and the rest is easier.

## Start with the job, not the title

In Italy, titles carry weight. A "team lead", a "manager" and a "head of" mean different things to candidates, and they can affect contract classification and the collective agreement that applies. Do not borrow a title from your home market and hope it fits.

Write down what the person will actually do in the first months:

- Which work do they own?
- Which decisions can they make alone?
- Who do they report to, and in which time zone?
- Will they manage people on day one, or build the team first?

If you cannot answer these in a few lines, the role is not ready to be advertised.

## Two common profiles

Most companies need one of two people. Be honest about which one you are hiring.

**The player-coach.** This person does the work and guides a small group. They suit a team that is still being built. They are hands-on, they write the processes as they go, and they are comfortable with a loose structure. The risk is overload. If you give them too much delivery work, the leadership part never happens.

**The people manager.** This person spends most of their time on hiring, feedback, planning and coordination. They suit a team that already has some delivery capacity. The risk is distance. If they have no technical or commercial credibility, the team will not follow them.

Mixing the two without saying so is the most common mistake. The candidate hears one job in the interview and finds another after they start.

## What to look for

A good first team lead in Italy usually shows these traits:

- **Experience with foreign headquarters.** They have worked with managers abroad, in English, across time zones. They know how to translate between your culture and the local one.
- **A local network.** They know where to find good people and who to avoid. This matters because your first hires will mostly come through them.
- **Comfort with ambiguity.** You have no local entity, no established routines and maybe no office. They need to act without a manual.
- **Credibility with the team.** They have done the work themselves. Italian teams tend to respect competence shown, not authority claimed.
- **Clear written English.** Your reporting lines, goals and feedback will pass through them. Weak English creates silent misunderstandings.

A long list of past titles is less useful than a short list of teams they built and results they can describe.

## Define the scope in writing

Before the first interview, prepare a short scope document. Keep it to a page. It should cover:

1. **Mission.** What the team must achieve in plain terms.
2. **Authority.** Hiring, budget, tools, customer contact. Say what is allowed and what needs approval from headquarters.
3. **Reporting line.** Name the person abroad who manages them, and how often they will talk.
4. **Growth path.** Whether the team is expected to grow, and what happens to the lead's role if it does.
5. **Success measures.** Two or three outcomes you will review together, agreed in advance.

Share this document with finalists. Good candidates will ask sharp questions about it. Weak ones will not read it.

## Decide how much authority to give

Foreign companies often hire a local lead and then keep every decision at headquarters. The lead becomes a messenger. Strong people leave quickly in that situation.

The opposite error also happens. A company gives full freedom, then reacts badly when the first decision differs from the home market approach.

A workable middle path: give clear authority over daily work and hiring within an agreed plan. Keep strategy, pricing and brand with headquarters. Review the split together after the early months and widen it as trust grows.

## Plan the legal side before the offer

You do not need a local company to hire in Italy. An Employer of Record can employ the person on your behalf while you direct their daily work. This lets you test the market and the person before committing to a subsidiary.

Still, settle the contract basics before you make an offer: the role level, the applicable collective agreement, working hours, remote arrangements and the notice terms. Candidates in Italy expect these details to be clear early. Vagueness reads as risk.

## A short checklist

- The role is described as player-coach or people manager, not both.
- The scope fits on one page.
- The reporting line and meeting rhythm are named.
- Authority limits are written down.
- The contract framework is clear before the offer.

Hire slowly for this role. One strong team lead makes every later hire cheaper, faster and better.
