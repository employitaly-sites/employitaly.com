# Rome beyond government: hiring in the capital

> Rome is more than ministries. See the sectors and talent pools that matter for foreign employers, plus the local payroll point to plan for.

Most foreign companies think of Rome as the city of ministries. That is part of the picture. It is not the whole picture. If you plan to hire in the capital, look at the wider labour market first.

## A city with many employer types

Rome hosts the national government, so public bodies and their suppliers are a large part of local work. Around them sits a deep layer of private activity. Consulting firms, law firms, audit teams and public affairs advisers cluster near the institutions. They need people who can read regulation and explain it to clients. That skill set is easy to find here.

International organisations and embassies add another layer. They bring multilingual staff, and many of those staff later move to the private sector. For a foreign company, this means a steady pool of candidates who already work across languages and cultures.

Then there are the sectors people forget:

- **Media and film.** Rome has a long production tradition. Studios, post-production houses and broadcasters employ editors, producers, sound engineers and animators.
- **Tourism and hospitality.** The city is a global destination. Hotels, tour operators and travel platforms need revenue managers, sales staff and customer teams.
- **Technology and digital services.** Software firms, start-ups and IT consultancies are present. Many serve public and corporate clients, so experience with large, regulated projects is common.
- **Life sciences and health.** Hospitals, research centres and universities feed a pipeline of clinical, regulatory and laboratory talent.
- **Aerospace, defence and engineering.** Large industrial groups and their suppliers sit in and around the city. Engineers and project managers with long experience are available.
- **Energy and infrastructure.** Utilities, engineering firms and construction groups run major projects from Rome.

## Where the talent comes from

Rome has large universities and many specialised schools. Graduates come out in law, economics, engineering, medicine, communication and the humanities. Language skills are strong in many of these groups.

The city also draws professionals from the rest of Italy and from abroad. People move here for the work and for the lifestyle. This helps when you need senior hires or niche profiles, because the pool is wider than the local population.

Two profiles stand out for foreign employers. The first is the regulatory and institutional specialist. This person knows how decisions are made and who to talk to. The second is the bilingual client-facing professional. Sales, account management and support roles benefit from the city's international character.

## What to check before you hire

Do not copy a hiring plan from another city. Sector clusters differ. A company that needs factory-floor staff may do better elsewhere. A company that needs advisers, creatives, researchers or institutional contacts will find Rome a strong base.

Test the market before you commit. Ask these questions:

- Which sector do your future colleagues come from today?
- Do you need people close to institutions or clients in the capital?
- Can the role work in a hybrid model?
- Do you need a senior profile that is rare, or a team you can grow?

## A local payroll point

Payroll in Italy has a local layer. Municipalities can apply a surcharge on personal income tax. For Rome, the source we use lists a single rate of 0.9%, with an exemption for income up to EUR 14,000. This applies to the employee and is handled through payroll. Treat the figure as a planning reference and confirm it when you set up your first contract, because local rules can change.

The point for employers is simple. Net pay depends on where the employee lives and is taxed. Build this into your offer so the candidate sees a clear figure. Surprises at the first payslip damage trust.

## Getting started without an entity

You do not need a local company to hire in Rome. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ the person on your behalf under Italian rules. You direct the work. The EOR handles the contract, payroll, social contributions and compliance. This lets you test the city with one hire, then grow if it works.

This route suits companies that want to move fast. It also suits teams that are not sure how long they will stay in Italy. You can start small and adjust.

Rome rewards employers who look past the obvious. Match your role to the right sector cluster, plan for local payroll costs, and use a setup that lets you start quickly.
