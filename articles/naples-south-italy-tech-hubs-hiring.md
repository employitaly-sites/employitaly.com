# Naples and the South: rising tech hubs for international employers

> Why companies look at Naples and Southern Italy for tech hires, what talent to expect, and how local tax and employer costs fit into the decision.

Most foreign companies hiring in Italy start in Milan or Rome. Naples and the South deserve a look too. Tech teams are growing there, universities feed local talent, and living costs are lower than in the northern cities. This article covers what to weigh before you open a hub or hire your first person in the area.

## Why the South is on the map

Naples is the largest city in the South. It has several universities, a large student population and a growing circle of software, digital and engineering companies. Other cities in the region follow a similar pattern: a strong academic base, a young workforce and fewer employers competing for the same people.

For an international company, this has practical effects:

- Candidates often stay longer with an employer that offers a serious technical role.
- Competition for senior engineers is less intense than in the large northern hubs.
- Remote and hybrid work is accepted, so you can hire from the South for a team based elsewhere.

None of this means hiring is easy. Some profiles are rare, and you still need a clear role, a fair salary and a plan for career growth. But the pool is real, and many companies have not looked at it yet.

## What kind of talent you can find

Expect strong profiles in software development, data, cybersecurity, embedded systems and industrial engineering. Many candidates speak good English and have worked with international clients or on remote teams. Some have studied or worked abroad and returned home. Others never left and prefer to build a career near family.

This last point matters. People who choose to stay in the South often value stability. A stable contract, clear terms and reliable payroll count for a lot. If you offer that, you can build a loyal team.

## Incentives: set expectations carefully

Southern Italy is often described as a region with generous public support for hiring and investment. Programs exist at national, regional and EU level. They change often, depend on the profile of the hire and the company, and have eligibility rules and deadlines. We do not publish figures for them here because we only quote verified data.

Our advice is simple. Do not build your business case on an incentive you have not confirmed. Treat any support as an upside. Check eligibility for your specific case before you commit, and ask the person handling your payroll to do the same.

## One local cost to know: the municipal income tax surcharge

One item is easy to verify. Italian employees pay a municipal surcharge on personal income tax, set by each city. According to a secondary source, Naples applies a single rate of 1%, with an exemption for income up to 12,000 EUR.

This is a cost for the employee, not for the employer. Still, it affects take-home pay, and it is part of the net salary conversation with candidates. When you compare Naples with another city, compare the net result for the person, not only the gross figure. Rates and exemptions can change, so confirm them against the current local rules before you quote a net salary in an offer.

## How to hire without a local entity

You do not need to set up a company in Italy to hire in Naples. An Employer of Record (EOR) employs the person on your behalf under Italian law. You choose the candidate and direct the work. The EOR handles the contract, payroll, social contributions and compliance with the national collective agreement that applies to the role.

This suits the South well, for three practical reasons:

- You can test the market with one or two hires before deciding on a larger presence.
- You avoid the time and cost of setting up a legal entity and local administration.
- You keep the option to move to your own entity later, when the team is large enough.

The EOR can also help with relocation and visas if you bring in talent from outside the EU, and with social security representation when needed.

## A practical way to start

Begin with the role, not the city. Define what the person will do, what seniority you need and which time zone overlap you require. Then decide whether the role can be remote, hybrid or based in Naples.

Next, look at the full package. Salary is one part. Italian employment also includes mandatory contributions, a severance provision, paid leave and a thirteenth month paid in many sectors. A good EOR will give you the full employer cost before you make an offer, so there are no surprises.

Finally, plan the first months. Give the new hire a clear point of contact, a defined onboarding and a real project. People in the South who join international teams often become the anchor of a local group, drawing in friends and former classmates over time. Treat the first hire as the start of something larger.

## Summary

Naples and the South offer talent, a lower-pressure hiring market and a growing tech scene. Public incentives may help, but confirm them case by case. Local taxes such as the municipal surcharge affect net pay, so check them when you set salaries. An EOR lets you hire quickly and compliantly without opening an entity.
