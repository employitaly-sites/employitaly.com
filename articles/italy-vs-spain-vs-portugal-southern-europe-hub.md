# Italy vs Spain vs Portugal for a Southern Europe hub: talent, cost and time zone

> Choosing between Italy, Spain and Portugal for a Southern Europe hub? Compare talent depth, employment cost and time zone fit before you hire.

If you plan a hub in Southern Europe, Italy, Spain and Portugal are the obvious candidates. They share a Mediterranean business culture and good access to European markets. They differ in talent pools, cost profile and working hours. This article compares them on those points so you can match the country to the role.

No single country wins on every measure. The right answer depends on what you hire for, where your customers are and how fast you need to start.

## Talent

**Italy** has a deep and varied labour market. It is strong in engineering, manufacturing, design, fashion, food, automotive, industrial automation and life sciences. Many Italian professionals have specialist skills built inside long-standing industrial companies. If your product touches hardware, machinery, pharma or luxury goods, Italy gives you a pool that is hard to match in the region. Finance, legal and technical sales talent is also available in the large cities, especially in the north.

Italy is less uniform than the other two. Talent clusters around Milan, Turin, Bologna, Rome and a few other centres. Outside these, the pool for niche roles can be thin. Remote hiring helps, but you should plan your location around the roles.

**Spain** offers a large and young workforce with a strong base in sales, customer support, marketing and software. Madrid and Barcelona are established tech and startup hubs. Spanish is also a major global language, which makes Spain a natural base for Latin American coverage. If you need multilingual support or a commercial team that sells into Spanish-speaking markets, Spain is often the easiest choice.

**Portugal** has a smaller population and therefore a smaller pool, but it has built a reputation for software, shared services and customer operations. English levels are generally high, and Lisbon and Porto attract many international teams. The trade-off is competition. Many foreign companies already hire there, so strong candidates have options, and the market can feel crowded for popular profiles.

## Cost

Cost is more than salary. Employer contributions, mandatory benefits, notice rules and local collective agreements all shape what a hire really costs.

Portugal has traditionally been seen as the lower-cost option for salaries, though that gap has narrowed in the big cities where international demand has pushed pay up. Spain sits in a similar middle range for many roles. Italy often shows a higher overall cost of employment for the employer because of contribution levels and the weight of collective agreements, but pay for specialist and industrial roles reflects the skills involved.

Do not compare headline salaries alone. In Italy, the national collective agreement for your sector sets minimum pay, extra monthly payments, leave and notice terms. These rules apply to your hire whether you plan for them or not. The same logic holds in Spain and Portugal, where sector agreements and statutory benefits add to the base pay. The useful number is the total annual cost per employee, built from verified rates for your sector and role.

For a small team in a first market entry, the cost difference between the three is often smaller than the cost of hiring the wrong profile or waiting months to start. Speed and fit usually matter more than a small saving on salary.

## Time zone

Italy and Spain run on central European time. Their working days line up closely with Germany, France, Switzerland and the rest of continental Europe. Meetings with headquarters in those countries need no special planning.

Portugal keeps the same clock as the United Kingdom and Ireland. That puts it slightly behind Italy and Spain. For a UK-facing team, this is a real advantage. For a team that must overlap with the rest of continental Europe, the gap is small but visible, especially at the end of the day.

For North America, all three countries are on the earlier side of the transatlantic window, and Portugal gives a slightly better overlap with the American morning. For Asia and the Middle East, the later end of the European day is what you can share, and Italy and Spain are marginally better placed.

Spain has one more habit worth noting. Lunch and the working day often run later than in Italy or Portugal. If your customers expect early calls, check this against your sales rhythm.

## How to choose

Use a simple rule of thumb.

- Choose **Italy** when you need industrial, engineering, design or specialist technical skills, or when the Italian market is itself your target.
- Choose **Spain** when you need scale in sales, support or marketing, or when you want a base for Spanish-speaking markets.
- Choose **Portugal** when you build a software or operations team that works closely with the UK, and you accept a smaller pool and stronger competition for candidates.

Many companies end up with more than one country over time. A common path is to start where the first critical hire lives, then add a second location when the team grows.

## Starting without a local entity

Whatever you choose, setting up a legal entity takes time and ongoing admin. An Employer of Record (EOR) lets you hire locally under local law without that setup. The EOR is the legal employer, handles payroll, contributions and contracts, and you direct the daily work. This is useful when you want to test a market, hire a single key person or move fast. In Italy, an EOR can also support visas, relocation and social security representation for foreign staff.

The practical step is to define the roles first, then compare the total employment cost and the time to start in each country for those roles.
