Hidden Costs of Hiring in Italy: What Budgets Often Miss

Most foreign companies start with a gross salary and add a rough percentage for contributions. That gets you a number. It is rarely the real number.

Italy has a layered employment cost. Some layers are visible. Others only show up after the first payroll run. This article lists the items that most often go missing from budgets.

Start with the contract, not the salary  

In Italy, the national collective agreement (CCNL) for your sector drives much of the cost. It sets minimum pay, extra monthly payments, leave rules, notice periods and allowances. Two employees with the same gross salary can cost very different amounts under different agreements.

Budget from the agreement that applies to the role. Do not budget from a salary figure alone. Ask which agreement applies before you make an offer.

Workplace injury insurance  

Every employer in Italy pays workplace injury insurance to INAIL. Many foreign teams do not know it exists until they see the line on a payroll report.

The cost is not flat. It depends on the risk class of the work. An office role and a field role sit in different classes and pay different premiums.

There is also a point that confuses even local advisers. The rate tables in force date from 2019. The rules on how they are applied changed in 2026, including how reductions for good safety records work. So a premium estimate based on an older article or an older quote may be wrong. Ask for a current calculation for the actual role.

Severance accrual  

Italian employees build up a severance entitlement, known as TFR, during employment. The employer sets this aside every year. It is paid when the contract ends, for any reason, including resignation.

This is a real liability from the first month. If your budget treats severance as a cost that only appears at dismissal, it is understated. Treat it as a monthly cost from day one.

Extra pay and accrued leave  

Many Italian contracts include additional monthly payments beyond the base salary. Foreign managers often read an annual salary and divide it evenly, then find the payroll is larger in certain months.

Accrued leave is another item. Unused days and paid hours do not disappear. They remain a liability on your books, and they are paid out if the employee leaves.

Check how your contract structures annual pay. Make sure the budget shows the cash profile by month, not only the annual total.

Payroll and compliance work  

Italian payroll is not a light task. Monthly processing, filings with social security and tax authorities, and year-end reporting all need local expertise. If you run payroll through a foreign provider, expect extra work to adapt it.

If you hire directly, you also need a local entity or a registration with the right authorities before the first employee starts. That means setup time, local advisers and fees before there is any revenue from the hire.

Health and safety obligations  

Italian employers carry legal duties on workplace safety. These include risk assessments, training, a designated safety role and medical checks for some roles. Remote workers are not exempt from every duty.

These are not large costs for a small team. They are easy to forget, and the penalties for missing them are real. Put them in the budget as a fixed annual item.

Visas and relocation  

If the person is not an EU citizen, work authorization adds time and cost. There are government fees, document preparation, translation and legal support. There may also be a delay between the offer and the start date, during which you pay for the process but get no work.

Relocation is similar. Housing search, registration with local offices and tax residency setup all take effort. Many companies cover some of it to win the candidate. Decide the policy before the offer, not after.

Termination costs  

Ending an employment relationship in Italy is regulated. Notice periods, procedures and possible disputes all matter. You should not assume that ending a contract is quick or free.

You do not need to budget for a dismissal in every case. You should know the likely cost range before you hire, so that the decision to hire is made with the full picture.

Time and management cost  

The last item is internal. Someone on your side will spend time on contracts, questions from the employee, local holidays, and coordination with advisers. In a small company, that someone is often a founder or a senior manager.

This cost does not appear on any invoice. It is still a cost.

A simple way to budget  

Build the budget in layers:

  • Base salary under the applicable collective agreement.
  • Employer social contributions.
  • Workplace injury insurance for the role’s risk class.
  • Monthly severance accrual.
  • Extra monthly pay and leave liability.
  • Payroll, compliance and safety obligations.
  • Visa and relocation, if needed.
  • A reserve for termination.

Ask for each layer in writing, with the source and the date. Rules and rates change. A figure with no date is a risk.

Why this matters  

The goal is not to scare you away from hiring in Italy. Italy has strong talent and a stable legal framework. The goal is to avoid a budget that looks fine in the board deck and fails in the first quarter.
