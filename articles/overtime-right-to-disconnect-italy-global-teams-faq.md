# Overtime and the Right to Disconnect for Italian Staff in Global Teams

> FAQ for foreign employers: how overtime, availability and the right to disconnect work for Italian employees who collaborate across time zones.

Your team is spread across time zones. Your new hire sits in Italy. Meetings land early or late for someone, and messages arrive at all hours. This FAQ covers what you can expect, and what you cannot, when an Italian employee works in a global team.

## Does Italian employment law care about working hours?

Yes. Working hours in Italy are set by law and by the collective agreement (CCNL) that applies to the role. The agreement sets normal weekly hours, rest periods, and how extra hours are counted and paid. Your contract can add detail. It cannot remove protections the law or the CCNL gives.

This surprises many foreign employers. In some countries, salaried staff are simply expected to work until the job is done. In Italy, hours are a legal topic, and the applicable CCNL decides much of the detail.

## What counts as overtime?

Overtime is work beyond the normal hours defined by law or by the CCNL. The agreement usually says how much overtime is allowed, how it must be requested, and how it is paid or compensated, for example with extra pay or time off.

A call at the end of the day that runs long is overtime if it pushes the employee past normal hours. It does not matter that the work is remote or that the call is "just a quick sync".

## Can I ask my Italian employee to attend calls outside local office hours?

Sometimes. A few early or late calls, agreed in advance, are normal in global teams. Problems start when the pattern becomes regular and nobody tracks it.

Good practice:

- Agree the expected working window in the contract or in a written policy.
- Rotate inconvenient meeting times so one office does not carry the burden.
- Record the extra time and compensate it as the CCNL requires.
- Ask first. Do not assume availability.

## Is overtime always paid?

Not always in the same way. Some roles, especially senior or managerial ones, may be treated differently under their CCNL or contract. But you should not assume that a job title removes the right to compensation or rest. Check the applicable agreement before you decide. A payroll mistake here can turn into a claim later.

## What is the right to disconnect?

It is the employee's right to stop work and not be reachable outside working hours, without suffering consequences. In Italy, this idea is linked to remote and flexible work arrangements. The individual agreement for remote work should state the employee's rest periods and the technical and organisational measures that allow them to disconnect.

In practice, it means your Italian employee can switch off after hours. They can ignore a late message until the next working day. You cannot mark that down as a lack of commitment.

## Does this apply if the employee works fully from home?

Yes. Working from home does not cancel working time rules. If anything, it makes a written agreement more important. The agreement should say when the employee is expected to be available, how work is measured, and how rest is protected.

## What about urgent issues, like a production outage?

True emergencies happen. Plan for them, do not improvise.

- Define in writing what counts as urgent.
- Set up an on-call arrangement if you need one, and check how the CCNL treats it.
- Give compensation or time off for work done outside normal hours.
- Keep emergencies rare. If every week is an emergency, you have a staffing problem, not an urgency problem.

## How do I manage expectations across time zones?

Set the rules early and put them in writing. A few habits help:

- **Publish core overlap hours.** Choose a window when the whole team is online and keep meetings inside it.
- **Default to asynchronous work.** Use written updates, recorded demos and shared documents so decisions do not wait for a live call.
- **Mark message urgency.** Make it clear what needs a reply today and what can wait.
- **Do not reward constant availability.** If the fastest replier gets praise, others will copy them and burn out.
- **Respect local holidays and rest.** Italian staff expect real time off. Do not schedule work into it.

## Can I monitor my Italian employee's hours?

Tracking working time is reasonable and often useful, since it shows overtime. But tools that monitor employee activity are regulated in Italy, and remote control of workers is limited. Do not install monitoring software without advice. Simple self-reported timesheets or agreed work logs are usually safer than intrusive tracking.

## What happens if I ignore all this?

The risks are practical. The employee may claim unpaid overtime. They may challenge a dismissal tied to a refusal to work after hours. Morale also drops quickly when staff feel they are always on call. Good employees leave. Replacing them costs more than respecting the rules.

The EOR makes sure that overtime is handled correctly, that remote work agreements include disconnection terms, and that the contract matches what you actually expect.

## Quick checklist

- Identify the CCNL for the role.
- Write the expected working window into the contract.
- Agree how extra hours are requested and compensated.
- Add disconnection terms to the remote work agreement.
- Rotate meeting times across offices.
- Use asynchronous work as the default.
- Plan emergencies in advance.
