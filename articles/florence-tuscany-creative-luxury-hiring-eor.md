# Hiring in Florence and Tuscany for creative and luxury roles

> Why Florence and Tuscany suit creative and luxury roles, and how an Employer of Record lets a foreign company hire there without its own Italian entity.

Florence and Tuscany have a long record in leather, fashion, textiles, jewelry, craft and design. If your company sells luxury goods or builds a creative team, this region is a natural place to hire. The skills are here. The supplier networks are here. The question is how to employ people there without opening a company in Italy.

This article explains where the region fits, which roles tend to work well, and how an Employer of Record (EOR) handles the employment side.

## Why the region fits

Tuscany is known for workshops and small manufacturers that serve major fashion houses. Many skills are passed on inside these workshops and are hard to find elsewhere. Florence adds design schools, a strong tradition in art and restoration, and a international visitor base that keeps retail and hospitality sharp.

For a foreign company, this means you can find people who understand materials, finishing and quality control at a high level. You can also find people who combine that craft knowledge with modern design and digital work.

## Roles that tend to work well

- **Product development and pattern making.** Technicians who turn a sketch into a producible item.
- **Leather goods and accessories specialists.** Sourcing, sampling and quality checks.
- **Textile and materials experts.** Fabric selection, supplier relations and testing.
- **Brand and visual designers.** Creative staff who can work with a remote head office.
- **Retail and client experience staff.** Multilingual people who serve international clients.
- **Production coordinators.** People who manage the link between your brand and local workshops.
- **Restoration and conservation specialists.** Useful for heritage brands and design-led companies.

Many of these roles can be remote or hybrid. Others need a person near the workshops, which is where a local employee makes the most sense.

## The practical problem for a foreign company

You may want one product developer near your suppliers. You may want a small design team. You do not want to set up a local company, open a bank account, register with the authorities and run payroll just to start.

An EOR solves this. The EOR is the legal employer in Italy. You choose the person and direct the daily work. The EOR handles the contract, payroll, social contributions, tax withholding and the required registrations.

## What the EOR does for you

**Contract and collective agreement.** Italian employment is shaped by national collective agreements for each sector. Fashion, leather, textiles and retail each have their own rules on pay grades, working time, leave and notice. The EOR picks the right agreement and builds the contract around it.

**Payroll and contributions.** Salaries are paid on time, with correct withholding and social contributions. You receive a clear monthly cost report.

**Compliance.** Registrations, mandatory communications and record keeping are done for you. You do not need to learn the rules or hire a local administrator.

**Visas and relocation.** If the person you want lives outside the EU, the EOR can support the work visa process and help with the move. This matters for senior designers, creative directors and specialists you want to bring to Florence.

**Social security representation.** If you employ people in Italy, someone must represent you before the social security bodies. The EOR takes that role.

## Planning a creative or luxury hire

Some points help you start well.

1. **Define the role in terms of the agreement.** A pattern maker, a designer and a shop assistant fall under different classifications. A clear job description speeds up the contract.
2. **Decide where the person works.** Close to the workshops, in Florence, or remote from elsewhere in Italy. Each choice affects the setup.
3. **Plan for language.** Craft staff often speak Italian first. Brand and retail staff usually add English and other languages. Say which you need.
4. **Think about intellectual property.** Designs, patterns and creative work should be assigned to your company in the contract. The EOR can include the right clauses.
5. **Allow time for onboarding.** Italian contracts have formal steps. Starting early avoids delays.

## Is an EOR the right tool?

An EOR suits you if you want to test the market, hire a small number of people, or move fast. It also suits companies that do not plan a permanent entity in Italy. If you later grow into a full local company, the team can be transferred.

For luxury and creative companies, the benefit is simple. You get access to a deep pool of skills without taking on the administration. Your people spend their time on product and brand. The EOR handles the paperwork.

Florence and Tuscany will not suit every role. Pure software or finance teams may find better matches in other Italian cities. But for craft, design, materials and client-facing work, the region is hard to beat.
