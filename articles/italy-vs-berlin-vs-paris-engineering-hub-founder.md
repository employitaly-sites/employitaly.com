# Italy vs Berlin vs Paris for an Engineering Hub: A Founder's View

> Choosing where to build your engineering hub? A founder's comparison of Italy, Berlin and Paris on talent, cost, speed to hire and how you enter each market.

You have decided to build an engineering hub in Europe. The shortlist is Berlin, Paris and Italy. Most founders pick the first two by habit. Italy deserves a real look.

This is not a ranking. It is a way to think about the choice as the person who has to live with it.

## Start with what the hub is for

Before comparing cities, answer one question. What will this team do in the first year?

If you need a large group of senior engineers fast, you are buying access to a deep, competitive market. If you need a small, stable core that stays for years, you are buying retention and fit. These are different problems. They point to different places.

## Berlin: the crowded market

Berlin is where many international founders go first. English works at the office. Engineers expect to work for foreign companies. The startup scene is mature.

That is also the downside. Every well-funded company in Europe recruits there. Strong candidates have options and know it. Offers move quickly. Counter-offers are common. Turnover can hurt a young hub.

Berlin suits you if you need brand visibility, want to hire many people, and can pay to compete. It is harder if you are a small foreign company nobody has heard of.

## Paris: strong talent, strong structure

Paris has deep engineering talent, especially in mathematics, machine learning and hardware-adjacent fields. Large tech employers and a well-funded ecosystem sit there. Engineers are well trained and ambitious.

France is also known for a heavily structured employment framework. Contracts, notice rules and employee protections are detailed. For a founder, this means you need local knowledge before the first hire, not after. A mistake in the first contract is expensive to fix.

Paris suits you if you want top-tier technical depth and are ready to respect a rigid system.

## Italy: the less contested option

Italy is the market founders overlook. That is the opportunity.

The country has a large pool of trained engineers. Many of them work in cities where few international companies hire directly. Engineers there often get fewer offers from foreign firms. A serious offer from a real product company stands out. People tend to stay when the work is good and the team is solid.

The trade-offs are real. Fewer candidates know English-first startups. Local employment rules, collective agreements and social contributions are specific. Hiring needs patience at the start, and you cannot copy a Berlin playbook.

Italy suits you if you want a stable team, lower competition for each role, and a place where your company can be a top employer from day one.

## The real founder question: who is the employer?

Talent is only half the decision. The other half is how you employ people.

To hire in any of these countries, you normally need a local entity. That means incorporation, payroll, tax registration, accounting and a legal representative. It takes time. It adds fixed cost before you know if the hub will work.

An Employer of Record (EOR) removes that step. The EOR becomes the legal employer. You manage the work. They handle contracts, payroll, social contributions and compliance. You can start hiring in weeks and decide on your own entity later, once the team is proven.

For a founder testing a market, this matters more than the city. It lets you run a real pilot instead of a bet.

## How to decide

Use this short test.

- **You need volume and speed, and have budget:** Berlin.
- **You need deep technical specialists and accept structure:** Paris.
- **You want a stable core team and less bidding pressure:** Italy.

Then add a second filter. Where do your first hires already live? Where does your head of engineering want to build? A hub led by someone who believes in the location will outperform a hub chosen on a spreadsheet.

Also consider a staged plan. Start with a small team in one country through an EOR. Learn how hiring, payroll and management feel. Add a second location only when the first works. Many hubs fail because the founder opened too many fronts at once.

## What to check before you commit

- Which collective agreement applies to your engineers, and what does it require?
- What are the total employment costs beyond salary?
- How long does a normal notice period last, and who bears it?
- Can you sponsor a visa if your best candidate lives outside the EU?
- Who handles relocation if you want to bring in talent?

Ask these before you write a single offer. The answers differ by country, and they change your real cost and risk.

## Bottom line

Berlin is the obvious choice and the most contested. Paris offers depth and rigidity. Italy offers quieter competition and loyal teams, if you accept a learning curve on local rules.

A founder does not need the perfect city. You need a place where you can hire well, keep people, and move without building a legal structure first.
