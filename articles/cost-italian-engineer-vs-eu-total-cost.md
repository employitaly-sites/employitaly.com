# What an Italian engineer really costs compared with other EU countries

> Gross salary is a poor way to compare engineers across the EU. Here is how to frame total employer cost, starting with Italy's pension contribution.

Comparing engineers across EU countries by gross salary is a common mistake. The salary is what the employee sees. It is not what the company pays. The number that matters for a budget is the total cost of employment.

This article explains how to frame that comparison. It also gives the one verified Italian figure we can stand behind today.

## Start with total cost, not salary

Total cost is the gross salary plus everything the employer pays on top of it. That includes social security contributions, insurance premiums and statutory deferred pay. It also includes the cost of running payroll and compliance.

Two countries can show the same gross salary and very different total cost. Two countries can also show different salaries and end up close. If you compare only the salary line, you compare the wrong thing.

## The Italian pension contribution

Italy funds old age, invalidity and survivors' pensions through the IVS contribution. The employer share of IVS is 23.81% of taxable pay, according to a secondary source we checked (centrofiscale.com). We list it as a secondary source, not an official INPS table, so confirm it against current INPS guidance before you budget.

This is one line of the employer cost. It is not the whole of it. Italian employers pay other contributions too, and the full set depends on the sector, the company size and the national collective agreement that applies. We do not quote those here because we have not verified them.

The point is simple. A large share of the Italian employer cost sits in contributions calculated on top of salary. Any comparison has to include them.

## How to compare Italy with other EU countries

Use the same method for every country on your shortlist. Do not mix methods.

**Pick the same role.** A senior backend engineer in one market should be compared with a senior backend engineer in another. Seniority bands differ, so define the role by what the person does.

**Use gross salary as the base.** Take a realistic market salary for the role in each country. Use recent offers or salary surveys from a source you trust.

**Add employer contributions.** For each country, find the employer social security rate and the base it applies to. Note any caps. Note any reduced rates for specific cases. Do this with official sources.

**Add the statutory extras.** Some countries require extra payments or accruals. Others do not. Ask what the employer must pay beyond salary and contributions, and whether some of it is paid later.

**Add the running cost.** Payroll, benefits administration, legal support and local registrations all cost money. In a country where you have no entity, this cost is real and often skipped.

**Compare the totals.** Only now put the countries side by side.

## What the comparison usually shows

In our experience the ranking by salary and the ranking by total cost rarely match. A country with a lower salary can cost more once contributions and extras are added. A country with a higher salary can be cheaper than it looks.

For Italy, the contribution load is a real part of the picture. A figure like the 23.81% IVS share shows why you cannot read the salary alone. It is also why you should not assume the cost is similar to a neighbouring country because the salaries look alike.

We will not put numbers on other countries here. The facts we have verified cover Italy's IVS share only. A fair cross-country table needs verified figures for every market, each with a source and a date.

## Cost is not the only factor

Total cost is one input. A good hiring decision also looks at the talent pool, time to hire and the notice and termination rules. It also looks at how fast you can start.

The last point is where many foreign companies lose time. Setting up an Italian entity, registering with the social security bodies and running payroll takes effort. If you only need one or a few engineers, that overhead can outweigh the saving you were chasing.

## A short checklist

- Define the role and seniority.
- Set a market gross salary.
- Add employer contributions from official sources.
- Add statutory extras and running costs.
- Compare totals, not salaries.
- Check the speed and the effort of starting in each country.
