# Hiring fintech and banking talent in Milan: roles and competition

> Which fintech and banking roles foreign companies hire in Milan, who you compete with for candidates, and how an Employer of Record helps you start fast.

Milan is the main financial centre in Italy. Banks, insurers, asset managers, payment firms and fintech startups all sit in the same city. For a foreign company, this means a deep pool of skilled people. It also means you will not be the only employer looking for them.

This article covers which roles you are likely to hire, who you compete with, and what that means for how you approach the market.

## Roles foreign companies usually hire

Most international fintech and banking firms do not open a full branch on day one. They start with a small team that serves the local market or supports a regional hub. The roles tend to fall into a few groups.

**Engineering and data.** Backend engineers, mobile developers, data engineers and data scientists. These people build payment flows, lending products, fraud models and reporting tools. Candidates with experience in regulated environments are in demand.

**Risk, compliance and legal.** Compliance officers, anti-money-laundering analysts, credit risk analysts and regulatory reporting specialists. Anyone who knows how Italian and European rules apply in practice is valuable. This group is hard to hire because the knowledge is local.

**Sales and partnerships.** Relationship managers, enterprise sales leads and partnership managers. Italian banks and corporates prefer to deal with someone who speaks the language and knows how decisions are made inside Italian institutions.

**Product and operations.** Product managers, customer operations leads and onboarding specialists. These roles connect your product to Italian customers and to local partners.

**Finance and treasury.** Finance managers and controllers who handle local accounting, tax and reporting for the new entity.

## Who you compete with

Candidates in Milan have many options. You should plan for that from the start.

**Large banks and insurers.** They offer stability, structured careers and well-known names. Many senior people have spent years in these institutions and value that security.

**Local fintech scale-ups.** These offer faster growth, equity and a product culture. They compete directly with you for engineers and product people.

**Other international firms.** Global banks, payment companies and technology groups already run offices in Milan. They hire from the same people you do, and many offer English-speaking teams and international mobility.

**Consulting firms.** They attract analysts and risk specialists with training and a clear path up.

In practice, strong candidates often hold more than one offer. Speed matters. A slow process loses people to employers who move first.

## What candidates look for

Salary is only part of the decision. Italian professionals also look at the following.

- **A legal employer in Italy.** Many candidates want a proper Italian employment contract, with local benefits, social security and clear rights. An offer through a foreign contract, or a vague contractor arrangement, makes some people hesitate.
- **Clarity on the role.** They want to know who they report to, what the team looks like and whether the company will stay in Italy.
- **Flexibility.** Hybrid work is common in this sector. Fully remote arrangements are less standard for regulated roles.
- **Career path.** A small foreign team can feel risky. Show how the role will grow.

If you can answer these points early, you improve your position against larger and better-known employers.

## The hiring challenge for a foreign company

You may want to hire in Milan before you have an Italian entity. Setting one up takes time. You also need payroll, social security registration, a compliant contract based on the right national collective agreement, and local HR support. Banking and financial roles often fall under specific collective agreements, and getting the contract wrong creates problems later.

You also face a timing issue. The best candidates will not wait while you complete incorporation.

For fintech and banking hiring, this gives you several practical advantages.

You can move to your own entity later if the team grows. The people you hired can transfer across.

## A simple approach

Start with a clear list of the first few roles. Decide which ones need to be in Milan and which can be remote. Prepare a contract and benefits package that matches what local employers offer. Move quickly once you find a good candidate. Use an EOR to employ them while you decide on a longer-term structure.

Milan rewards companies that are prepared. The talent is there. The competition is real. A fast, clear and compliant offer is your best tool.
