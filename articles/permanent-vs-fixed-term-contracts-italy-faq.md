# Permanent vs Fixed-Term Contracts in Italy: FAQ for Foreign Employers

> Permanent or fixed-term in Italy? Answers on what candidates prefer, how employers choose, and what to weigh before you make your first hire.

Foreign companies hiring in Italy often ask the same question. Should the first hire be permanent or fixed-term? The answer depends on the role, the market and your plans. This FAQ covers how candidates see each option and how employers usually decide.

## What is the basic difference?

A permanent contract has no end date. A fixed-term contract ends on an agreed date or when a defined task is done. Both give the employee a written contract, social security coverage and the protections of Italian labour law. The difference is mainly about how long the relationship is meant to last and how it can end.

## Which one do candidates prefer?

Most candidates prefer permanent contracts. In Italy, a permanent role signals stability. It helps people get a mortgage, rent a home and plan family life. Banks and landlords tend to treat it as the safe option.

This matters most for experienced professionals. Senior engineers, finance managers and sales leaders often have a choice of offers. If you offer a fixed-term contract to a candidate who has a permanent offer elsewhere, you will usually lose them.

There are exceptions. Some candidates accept fixed-term roles willingly:

- People early in their careers who want experience.
- Specialists who work project by project.
- Candidates moving from abroad who are unsure about staying long term.
- People between jobs who need income quickly.

If you want to attract strong talent, assume permanent is the default expectation.

## Why do employers choose fixed-term?

Employers choose fixed-term contracts when the need is temporary. Typical cases are a defined project, a market test, a seasonal peak or cover for an absent employee. It gives you a clear end point and lower long-term commitment.

It can also work as a trial of the Italian market. You can test demand before you build a permanent team.

## What are the downsides of fixed-term?

Italian rules limit how fixed-term contracts can be used and renewed. They may require a stated reason in some cases. If you use them wrongly, the contract can be treated as permanent. That risk is real, so do not treat fixed-term as a loophole.

There are also business costs:

- Higher turnover and repeat hiring effort.
- Lower commitment from staff who expect to leave.
- Weaker employer brand among candidates who want stability.
- Knowledge walking out of the door when the contract ends.

Check the current limits with a local adviser before you draft any fixed-term offer.

## Why do employers choose permanent?

Permanent contracts suit roles you expect to keep. They help you attract and retain people. They also signal that you are serious about the Italian market, which matters when you build a local reputation.

The trade-off is that ending a permanent contract in Italy is more regulated than in many other countries. You need a valid reason and a correct process. Foreign employers used to at-will employment are often surprised by this. It does not mean you cannot manage performance or restructure. It means you must follow the rules and document decisions.

## Is there a probation period?

Permanent contracts can include a probation period if it is agreed in writing before work starts. This gives both sides time to assess the fit. The details depend on the collective agreement that applies to the role, so check it before you set terms.

## Does the collective agreement matter?

Yes. Most Italian employment is shaped by a national collective agreement for the sector. It affects pay levels, notice, leave and other terms. The contract type you choose sits inside that framework. Pick the right agreement for your business and read it before you make offers.

## How should a foreign company decide?

Use a simple test:

- Is the need ongoing? Choose permanent.
- Is the need clearly temporary and defined? Fixed-term may fit.
- Is the candidate senior or in demand? Expect to offer permanent.
- Are you unsure about the market? Consider a fixed-term start, but plan the conversion path and respect the renewal rules.

Also think about who is the legal employer. If you have no Italian entity, you cannot simply issue an Italian contract yourself. An Employer of Record (EOR) can hire the person locally, run payroll and keep the contract compliant, while you direct the daily work. The same permanent or fixed-term choice applies. The EOR helps you apply it correctly.

## What is the best practice?

Match the contract to the real need. Do not use fixed-term to avoid commitment on a role that is clearly long term. Candidates notice, and the legal risk grows with each renewal.

Be open with candidates about the plan. If a role may become permanent, say so and explain the conditions. Clear expectations build trust and reduce early resignations.

Get the paperwork right from the start. A clean contract, the correct collective agreement and timely registrations prevent most disputes later.
