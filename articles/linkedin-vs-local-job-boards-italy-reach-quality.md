# LinkedIn vs Local Job Boards in Italy: Reach and Quality

> Where to post jobs when hiring in Italy: how LinkedIn and local job boards compare on reach and candidate quality, and how to combine them.

You have decided to hire in Italy. Now you need candidates. The first question is where to post the role. LinkedIn is the default for most international companies. Local job boards are the default for many Italian employers. They do not reach the same people.

This article compares them on reach and quality. It does not give market statistics. It gives a way to choose.

## What reach means here

Reach is not the size of a platform. Reach is how many suitable people in your target profile see your post and respond.

A large audience of the wrong people is noise. A smaller audience of the right people is a pipeline. Judge each channel by the people it puts in front of you for this specific role.

## LinkedIn: strengths

LinkedIn works well for roles that already live in an international setting.

- **Professional profiles.** You can see work history, skills and seniority before you contact anyone.
- **Direct search.** You can find people who have not applied and who are not looking actively.
- **English-language candidates.** If the role needs fluent English, LinkedIn is a natural filter.
- **Familiar to your team.** Your hiring managers abroad already use it, so the process is easy to run.

It suits software, product, finance leadership, sales leadership, and any role where the person will work with colleagues in other countries.

## LinkedIn: limits

LinkedIn does not cover the whole Italian labour market.

Many candidates in operational, technical, trade and administrative roles keep thin profiles or none at all. Some sectors rely on other channels. If you only post on LinkedIn, you may never see a large part of the people who could do the job.

Seniority also skews the picture. Experienced managers are well represented. Early-career and hands-on roles are less so.

Another risk is volume without fit. A visible post in English can attract many applicants who did not read the requirements. Screening takes time, and your time is the cost.

## Local job boards: strengths

Italian job boards are built for the local market and the local way of applying.

- **Local candidates.** People looking for work in Italy often start there, in Italian.
- **Wider range of roles.** Roles that are rare on LinkedIn, such as plant, field, retail and back-office work, are common on local boards.
- **Regional focus.** Many boards let candidates search by city or region. Italy is a country of regions, and people often want to stay in theirs.
- **Clear intent.** Someone browsing a job board is usually looking now.

## Local job boards: limits

The interface and the expectations are Italian. Your posting should be in Italian, and it should follow local conventions for job titles, contract type and working hours. A literal translation of a foreign job ad often reads strangely and gets fewer good responses.

Profiles are also less detailed than on LinkedIn. You often get a CV and a short form, and not much else. That means more work to judge fit.

Some boards attract a high number of unfiltered applications. Others are narrow and sector-specific. You need to know which is which before you pay or post.

## Quality: what to look at

Quality is not about which platform is better. It depends on the role.

Use these checks:

- **Language.** If daily work is in English, test English early. If daily work is in Italian, write the ad in Italian and expect Italian CVs.
- **Seniority.** Senior and specialist roles often come through LinkedIn search and referrals. Junior and operational roles often come through local boards.
- **Location.** If the person must be on site in a specific city, a board with regional search helps. If the role is remote within Italy, LinkedIn's wider net helps.
- **Passive vs active.** If the best people are already employed and not looking, you must search and approach them. That is LinkedIn work. If the pool is mostly active job seekers, boards perform well.

## A practical way to combine them

Most companies do better with both channels, each used for what it does best.

1. Write the role in Italian and in English. Keep the content consistent.
2. Post the Italian version on a local board that fits the sector.
3. Post the English version on LinkedIn, and search actively for profiles that match.
4. Use the same screening questions on both, so you can compare candidates fairly.
5. Track where each good candidate came from. After a few hires you will know which channel earns its place for each type of role.

## Do not forget the hiring side

Finding the person is only the start. In Italy you also need a compliant contract, the correct national collective agreement, payroll, social contributions and onboarding. A candidate who accepts an offer will expect these to be handled properly and on time.

If you do not have an Italian entity, an Employer of Record (EOR) can employ the person on your behalf while you direct their work. That lets you move from a good interview to a signed contract without setting up a company first.

## Summary

LinkedIn gives you reach among international, senior and English-speaking professionals, and the ability to approach people directly. Local job boards give you reach among local, active and regional candidates, especially for roles that are not visible on LinkedIn.

Choose by role, not by habit. Use both when the profile is unclear.
