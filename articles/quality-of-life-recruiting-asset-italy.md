# Quality of Life as a Recruiting Asset in Italy

> How food, climate, culture and pace of life help founders attract international talent to an Italian hub, and how to turn them into a hiring pitch.

Founders often ask how to attract strong people to a new hub. Salary matters. So does the work itself. But for many candidates, the city they will live in decides the offer. Italy has a real advantage here. This article explains how to use it.

## Why lifestyle matters in hiring

Skilled people can work in many places. Remote work widened their options. They now compare cities, not just jobs. They ask where they will spend evenings, weekends and holidays.

A company in Italy can answer that well. You do not need to invent a story. The country already has what many candidates want.

## What candidates actually notice

**Food and daily rhythm.** Fresh markets, local cafés and long lunches are part of normal life. People eat well without effort or a large budget. Candidates from busy, fast-paced cities often mention this first.

**Climate and landscape.** Mild seasons, sea, lakes, mountains and countryside are close to most Italian cities. A hiker, a skier and a beach lover can all live well in the same country. Time outdoors is easy to build into a week.

**Culture and history.** Museums, music, festivals and old city centers are part of the setting. You do not need to plan a trip to find them. They are on the way to the office.

**Pace and community.** Many Italian cities are walkable. Neighbors know each other. Family and friends matter. People who want a calmer life, without leaving a serious career, often find it here.

**Travel.** Italy sits in the middle of Europe and the Mediterranean. Short trips to other countries are easy. This helps people who want to stay close to family abroad.

## Which candidates respond best

Not everyone wants the same thing. Be honest about who you are trying to reach.

- Experienced professionals who want a better balance between work and life.
- People with young families who want space, safety and good food.
- Remote-first talent looking for a base with a strong culture.
- Specialists who want a hub with a smaller, closer team.

If you want a very fast, high-intensity startup scene, say so. Do not oversell the lifestyle to people who will not enjoy it. A good fit keeps people longer.

## How to use this in your recruiting

**Lead with the place, then the role.** In a job post, add a short paragraph about where the team lives and works. Name the city. Describe a normal week. Keep it factual.

**Show real people.** Ask current team members to share what they like. A short note from a colleague works better than a brochure. Candidates trust peers.

**Offer a visit.** If the budget allows, invite finalists to see the city and meet the team. One day in person often settles the decision.

**Support the move.** Lifestyle is only attractive if the move feels manageable. Candidates from abroad worry about visas, housing, registration and tax. If you can show a clear plan for these steps, the lifestyle pitch becomes believable. Without it, the same candidate sees risk.

**Be clear about trade-offs.** Italy has bureaucracy. Some processes are slow. Telling candidates early builds trust. Then explain who will help them through it.

## Lifestyle does not replace a good offer

Quality of life is an asset, not a substitute for fair pay and a good role. Treat it as the tie-breaker. When two offers are close, the place often wins. When your offer is weak, the place will not save it.

Also remember that lifestyle claims must match daily reality. If your office is hard to reach or your team works late every night, the pitch fails. Align the promise with how the team really works.

## The role of a local employment partner

A founder entering Italy usually does not have a local entity yet. That can slow hiring and make the move feel uncertain for candidates. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ people in Italy on your behalf. It handles contracts, payroll and social contributions under Italian rules. It can also support visas and relocation for people coming from abroad.

This matters for recruiting. A candidate who sees a clear path to a signed contract, a valid permit and a first day in a new city is more likely to say yes. You can focus on the team and the product. The legal and administrative work is handled by people who do it every day.

## A simple approach

1. Decide which kind of talent fits your hub.
2. Write a short, honest description of life in your city.
3. Let your team tell their own story.
4. Prepare a clear relocation and onboarding plan.
5. Keep the offer competitive, and let the place make the difference.

Italy gives founders something many hubs cannot: a place people want to live. Use it with care and with a plan to support the move.
