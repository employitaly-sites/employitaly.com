# Hiring in Smaller Italian Cities and Towns: Quality of Life and Retention

> Why hiring in smaller Italian cities and towns can improve quality of life and retention, and how an Employer of Record (EOR) makes it simple to do.

Most foreign companies look at Milan or Rome first. That is a fair starting point. But it is not the only one. Many international employers now hire in smaller Italian cities and towns, and they often see better retention as a result.

This article explains why, and what you should plan for.

## Why people stay in smaller places

People rarely leave a job only because of pay. They leave when daily life gets hard. Long commutes, high rents and crowded transport wear people down.

In a smaller city, much of that pressure is lower. Many people live close to the office or work from home. They can walk to the market, the school and the doctor. Lunch is a real break, not a rushed sandwich.

This matters for remote and hybrid roles. A person who has a calm home life and a stable community has fewer reasons to look elsewhere.

## Roots are a retention tool

Many Italians want to live near family. They want to stay in the town where they grew up, or return to it. A job that lets them do this is rare in some regions. When you offer it, you stand out.

An employee who can stay near parents, friends and a familiar home tends to feel loyal to the employer that made it possible. You do not need to buy this loyalty with perks. You offer a real option that larger employers in big cities cannot match.

This applies to your own expatriate staff as well. A relocated professional with a family may find a smaller town easier to settle in. Schools, housing and local services can be simpler to deal with. Settling in faster helps people stay longer.

## A wider pool of talent

If you only recruit in a few big cities, you compete with every other international company there. Candidates in those places get many offers.

In smaller cities, fewer employers recruit for remote or international roles. Skilled people there may have fewer choices. A well-run offer from a foreign company can be very attractive to them.

Many regions also have strong local universities and technical schools. They produce graduates in engineering, languages, design and other fields. Some of those graduates leave for the big cities, and some would rather not.

## What to plan for

Smaller places have trade-offs. Be honest about them in your hiring plan.

- **Fewer local peers.** A specialist may be the only person in their field in town. Plan regular contact with the wider team, online and in person.
- **Travel.** Some towns are far from airports and fast rail. If the role needs travel, check the routes first.
- **Connectivity.** Test internet quality before you commit to a fully remote role.
- **Career growth.** Make the path clear. People stay when they can see what comes next.
- **Local rules.** Employment rules apply across Italy, but the working culture can differ by region. Local knowledge helps.

None of these are reasons to avoid smaller towns. They are things to solve before the first day.

## How to run it without a local company

You do not need to open a legal entity in Italy to hire in a small town. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ the person on your behalf. The EOR handles the contract, payroll, social security and the other formalities. You direct the daily work.

This is useful when you want to test a location. You can hire one person in a town, see how it goes, and add more later. If the plan changes, you have not built a local structure that you must then close.

The EOR also applies the right national labour contract and local requirements, so you do not have to learn them yourself. The employee gets a compliant Italian contract with full rights. That clarity helps retention too, because people trust an employer that gets the basics right.

## Practical tips for retention

1. **Ask where people want to live.** Let the candidate tell you. Do not assume the capital is the best answer.
2. **Offer flexibility.** Remote or hybrid work is what makes a small-town hire possible.
3. **Plan team contact.** Schedule visits and shared events so nobody feels isolated.
4. **Respect local rhythms.** Holidays, family time and local festivals matter. Plan around them.
5. **Be consistent.** Pay on time, keep promises and communicate clearly.

## The bottom line

Hiring outside the big cities is not a compromise. For the right roles, it is a deliberate choice. It gives you a wider talent pool and employees who value the life they can build. That is a strong base for long tenure.

Start with one role. Pick a place that fits the work. Use an EOR so the legal side stays simple. Then review what you learn.
