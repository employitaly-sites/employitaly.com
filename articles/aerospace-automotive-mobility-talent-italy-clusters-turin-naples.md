# Aerospace, automotive and mobility talent in Italy: from Turin to Naples

> Where to find engineers and technicians for aerospace, automotive and mobility in Italy, from the Turin cluster to Naples, and how to hire them without an entity.

Italy has deep industrial know-how in vehicles, aircraft and the components that go into them. The talent is not in one place. It sits in clusters, each with its own history, schools and supplier networks. If you plan to hire here, knowing the map saves time.

This article walks through the main clusters from north to south. It then explains how a foreign company can hire in them without opening a local entity.

## Turin and Piedmont: automotive roots, growing aerospace

Turin is the historic home of Italian car making. Decades of design, powertrain and body engineering left a dense base of suppliers, test facilities and technical people. The polytechnic university feeds the region with engineers who know vehicle systems.

Today the same skills move into new areas. Electrification, software-defined vehicles, battery systems and advanced materials all draw on the Turin talent pool. The region also has a solid aerospace presence, which means engineers who understand certification, quality systems and long product cycles.

If you need vehicle engineers, homologation specialists or design professionals, start here.

## Lombardy and Emilia-Romagna: motors, machines and precision

Lombardy combines heavy manufacturing with strong engineering schools in Milan. Many tier-one and tier-two suppliers sit in this area. You will find mechanical, electronic and industrial engineers, plus a large base of skilled technicians.

Emilia-Romagna is known as Motor Valley. High-performance cars and motorcycles are built there. The area has people with rare skills in composites, engine development, aerodynamics and low-volume precision production. Packaging machinery and automation firms nearby add mechatronics and controls expertise.

These regions suit companies that need specialists in performance, lightweight structures or advanced manufacturing.

## Veneto, Friuli and the north-east: suppliers and mobility components

The north-east has many small and mid-size manufacturers. They supply parts, tooling and electronics to vehicle makers across Europe. The workforce is practical and production-minded. For firms that want to build a supplier team, a quality group or a manufacturing engineering function, this belt is worth a look.

## Lazio and Tuscany: space, defence and systems

Around Rome, the focus shifts to space systems, avionics, satellites and defence electronics. Research institutions and large aerospace groups shape the local market. Engineers here work on software, sensors, communications and systems integration.

Tuscany adds specialised mechanical and electronic firms, including makers of precision components for aircraft and rail.

## Campania and Naples: an aerospace base in the south

The south is often overlooked by foreign employers. That is a mistake in aerospace. Campania has a long tradition in aircraft structures, aero-engine parts and maintenance work. Naples has a university with strong aerospace engineering programmes. Industrial sites in the surrounding area handle composites, assembly and testing.

Puglia and neighbouring regions add to this, with aerospace production and growing research activity. Hiring in the south can give you access to skilled engineers in markets with less competition from other international employers.

## What this means for your hiring plan

Match the role to the cluster. Do not look for a motorsport composites engineer in a region known for aircraft maintenance. Do not expect to find a satellite software lead in a car-body hub.

A few practical points:

- Engineers in these sectors often prefer to stay near their family and their city. Remote or hybrid work from a cluster town is a strong offer.
- Technical Italian matters on the shop floor. Engineers in design and software roles usually work well in English.
- Supplier networks are tight. Reputation travels fast, so a clear and fair offer helps you in the next search.
- Salary expectations and contract terms follow national collective agreements. These vary by sector and must be applied correctly.

## Hiring without a local entity

Setting up a company in Italy takes time and local knowledge. For a small team, or a test of the market, it is often not worth it.

An Employer of Record (EOR) hires the person on your behalf under Italian rules. You choose the candidate, set the work and manage the day-to-day. The EOR handles the contract, payroll, social security and compliance. You can start a new engineer in Turin, Bologna or Naples without a branch or subsidiary.

If the hire comes from outside the EU, the EOR can also support the visa process and relocation. That is useful in aerospace and mobility, where specialists often move between countries.

When your team grows and the business case is clear, you can open your own entity later and transfer the staff.
