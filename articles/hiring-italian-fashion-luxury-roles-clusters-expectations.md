# Hiring in Italian Fashion and Luxury: Roles, Clusters and Expectations

> Where fashion and luxury talent sits in Italy, which roles are hard to fill, and what Italian employees expect from a foreign employer.

Italy is a base for fashion and luxury. Foreign brands, suppliers and design studios come here for craft skills, materials know-how and a dense network of specialist workshops. Hiring locally is the best way to tap that. It also means you need to understand how the sector is organised and what candidates expect.

This article covers the roles you are likely to hire, where the talent is, and what to plan for as a foreign employer.

## Roles that matter

The sector splits into creative, technical, commercial and support functions. Each has its own market.

**Creative and product development.** Designers, pattern makers, technical designers and prototype specialists. A pattern maker turns a sketch into something that can be cut and sewn. Good ones are scarce and often known by name in their district.

**Production and quality.** Production planners, quality controllers, supply chain coordinators and sourcing managers. These people manage suppliers, check finishes and keep collections on schedule. Many have worked inside the same supplier network for years.

**Craft and manufacturing.** Leather workers, tailors, knitwear technicians, footwear makers, jewellery setters. If you plan to run or control production in Italy, this is where hiring is hardest. Skills are learned over years, and training takes time.

**Retail and client relations.** Boutique managers, sales advisers, clienteling specialists, visual merchandisers. Luxury retail depends on language skills, product knowledge and a calm, polished manner with clients.

**Commercial and brand.** Wholesale managers, merchandisers, marketing and communications staff, e-commerce managers. These roles often sit in the larger cities and work closely with head office abroad.

**Support functions.** Finance, legal, HR and logistics. Fashion companies need the same back office as any other business, but with seasonal rhythms.

## Clusters

Talent is not spread evenly. The sector grew in districts, each with its own specialty.

- **Milan and Lombardy** are the centre for design, communication, showrooms, buying and corporate roles. Most international brands place their commercial and creative teams here.
- **Veneto** has strong footwear and eyewear traditions, with many specialist manufacturers.
- **Tuscany** is known for leather goods, tanning, textiles and high-end tailoring.
- **Marche and Emilia-Romagna** have footwear, knitwear and clothing producers, many of them family-owned.
- **Piedmont** is linked to fine textiles and menswear.
- **Rome** has a long couture and jewellery tradition and a growing number of brand offices.

If you need creative or commercial staff, Milan is the natural choice. If you need makers, go to the district where the craft lives. Moving a skilled artisan to another city is rarely realistic. Plan to hire where the skill already is.

## What candidates expect

Italian professionals in this sector are proud of their work. They expect a foreign employer to respect it.

**A proper contract.** Candidates expect a written employment contract with clear terms, and a sector collective agreement that sets minimum pay, working hours, leave and notice. Employment in Italy is regulated closely, and good candidates know their rights. A vague or informal offer will lose them.

**Stability.** Many prefer a permanent role. Fixed-term offers work for projects or seasonal peaks, but senior and technical staff look for long-term commitment.

**Benefits beyond salary.** Supplementary pension, health cover, meal vouchers and similar extras are common. Candidates compare packages, so check what is normal for the role.

**Clear reporting lines.** If the manager is abroad, say so and explain how decisions are made. Local staff dislike waiting for answers across time zones with no one empowered to decide.

**Respect for craft and timing.** Production calendars follow collections. Asking for rushed changes late in the cycle damages trust with workshops and suppliers.

**Language.** Italian is expected for most local roles. English is common in corporate and creative teams, but not in workshops.

## Practical points for foreign employers

You do not need an Italian company to hire here. An Employer of Record (EOR) can employ staff on your behalf, handle payroll, contributions and compliance, and let you manage the daily work. This suits brands testing the market, opening a showroom, or adding a few specialists without building a local entity first.

Whatever route you choose, do these things early:

- Define the role and the applicable collective agreement before you advertise.
- Check the real market pay for the role and the district.
- Decide who manages the person day to day.
- Set up onboarding that explains your brand, your standards and your calendar.
- If you hire from outside the EU, plan for visas and relocation well before the start date.

Italian fashion and luxury rewards employers who take the time to understand it. Hire in the right cluster, offer a clear and fair contract, and respect the craft.
