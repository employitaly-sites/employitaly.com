# How to Recruit Software Developers in Italy

> Where to find software developers in Italy, how long hiring takes, and how to pitch your role so strong candidates reply and accept.

Italy has a deep pool of software engineers. Many work for local firms, consultancies or foreign companies already. Hiring them from abroad is very possible. It just works differently from hiring in the US or the UK.

This guide covers where to look, what to expect on timing, and how to write a pitch that gets answers.

## Where developers are found

### Professional networks

LinkedIn is the main channel for mid and senior engineers. Most Italian developers keep a profile and many reply to a clear, personal message. Generic outreach gets ignored. A short note that names their stack and says why you picked them performs far better.

### Developer communities

Italy has active local communities around languages and frameworks. Meetups, Slack and Telegram groups, and conference audiences are full of people who care about their craft. Sponsoring a talk or answering questions in a group builds trust over time. Posting a job link cold does not.

### Open source and code platforms

GitHub shows real work. If you hire for a specific language, search for contributors based in Italy. Read their code before you write to them. Mention a project by name. That one detail sets you apart from every other message in their inbox.

### Universities and technical schools

Italian universities produce strong engineering graduates, especially in the big northern cities and in Rome, Naples and Turin. Contact career offices and research groups if you want junior talent. Many graduates are open to remote roles with foreign firms, because local pay for juniors can feel low to them.

### Referrals

The Italian tech scene is small. A good hire often knows other good people. Ask every developer you hire, and every candidate you reject politely, whether they know someone suitable. Referrals tend to close faster and stay longer.

## Timelines to expect

Hiring in Italy is rarely instant. Plan for these stages.

- **Sourcing and first contact.** Allow time for replies. Many candidates answer after a few days, not hours.
- **Interviews.** Keep the process short. A long chain of technical rounds loses people to faster offers. A single technical conversation plus a talk with the team lead is often enough.
- **Notice period.** This is the part foreign teams miss most. Italian employees usually have contractual notice periods with their current employer. For senior roles these can be long. Ask about notice early so your start date is realistic.
- **Contract and onboarding.** The employment contract must follow Italian rules. Payroll, social security registration and benefits must be ready before day one. If you have no Italian entity, this is where an Employer of Record (EOR) removes the delay. You do not need to set up a company first.

The practical rule: tell candidates the full timeline at the start. Surprises later cost you the hire.

## What to put in your pitch

Italian developers read a lot of vague job posts. A strong pitch is specific.

**State the role and stack in the first lines.** Name the language, the framework, the product and the team size. Skip filler about being a fast-growing family.

**Be clear about remote work.** Say if the role is fully remote, hybrid or tied to a city. Many candidates want remote work and will filter on it first.

**Show the salary range.** Pay transparency builds trust. If you hide it, you lose people who suspect it is low. Quote the gross annual figure and say if it is paid in the standard Italian structure.

**Explain the employment setup.** Candidates worry about foreign employers. Tell them they will have a proper Italian employment contract, with Italian social security, paid leave and the usual protections. Say who the legal employer is. When an EOR holds the contract, say so plainly and explain that you manage the daily work.

**Describe the work and the people.** Who will they report to? What will they build in the first months? Engineers care about technical quality, code review habits and how decisions get made.

**Respect language and time zones.** Most Italian developers work well in English. Say which language the team uses. Name the overlap hours you expect.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Italy as a low-cost market. Strong developers know their value and compare offers from across Europe.
- Asking for a long unpaid take-home test.
- Ignoring notice periods when you set a start date.
- Offering a freelance arrangement when the candidate wants a stable employment contract.
- Replying slowly. Good candidates often hold several conversations at once.

## A simple approach

Pick two or three channels and work them well. Write a pitch with the stack, pay, remote terms and employer details. Keep interviews short. Be honest about timing. Then use an EOR so the contract, payroll and compliance are in place when the candidate says yes.
