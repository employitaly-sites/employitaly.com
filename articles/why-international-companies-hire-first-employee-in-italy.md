# Why International Companies Hire Their First Employee in Italy

> Why foreign companies hire their first person in Italy: talent, market access, customer proximity and testing the ground before setting up a local entity.

Most foreign companies do not plan to "enter Italy". They start with a single hire. A sales lead, an engineer, a designer, a country manager. The reasons behind that first hire vary by sector, but a few patterns repeat.

This article describes those patterns. It does not promise a fit for every business. It shows what we see when companies decide to put a person on the ground.

## Access to talent

The most common reason is skills. A company needs a specific profile and finds that the best candidate lives in Italy.

This happens often in engineering, industrial design, fashion technology, food science, mechanical and automation work, and software. Italy has deep expertise in manufacturing, craft and design. Foreign companies that work in or near these fields want people who understand the product and the supply chain from the inside.

There is also a practical side. Many strong candidates prefer to stay in Italy for family or lifestyle reasons. A company that is open to hiring locally can reach people who would never relocate abroad. The company gets a good hire. The hire keeps their life where it is.

## Access to the Italian market

The second reason is customers. Italian buyers often prefer to deal with someone who speaks their language, knows local habits and can meet in person.

A remote team in another country can win some deals. But trust is built faster with a local face. A first hire in sales, business development or customer success gives the company a voice that Italian clients understand.

This matters most in sectors with long sales cycles or strong relationships. Think of industrial equipment, healthcare, professional services, hospitality technology and public sector suppliers. In these areas, local presence is often the difference between a reply and silence.

## Supplier and partner proximity

Some companies hire in Italy because their suppliers, manufacturers or partners are there. A quality manager or sourcing specialist near the factory can solve problems that email cannot.

This is common for brands that design abroad and produce in Italy. It is also common for technology firms that rely on Italian hardware makers. One person on site can check samples, follow deliveries and keep relations healthy.

## Testing the market before committing

Many companies want proof before they invest in a full local structure. Opening a subsidiary takes time and administrative work. It also creates ongoing obligations for accounting, payroll and compliance.

A first hire lets the company test demand with a small commitment. If sales grow, the team can grow. If the market is slower than expected, the company has not built a heavy structure around a guess.

This is where an Employer of Record (EOR) fits. The EOR employs the person in Italy on the company's behalf. The company directs the daily work. The EOR handles contracts, payroll, contributions and local rules. The foreign company does not need its own Italian entity to start.

## Speed

Timing often drives the decision. A company wins a client, sees an opportunity or finds the right candidate, and wants to move now. Waiting to set up an entity can mean losing the person or the deal.

A compliant way to employ someone quickly removes that bottleneck. The hire starts when the business needs them, not when the paperwork is finished.

## Following a key person

Sometimes the reason is simple. An existing employee or a trusted contractor lives in Italy, or wants to move there. The company wants to keep them, and a proper employment contract is the right way to do it.

In other cases, the company needs to bring someone from abroad to Italy. That adds visa and relocation steps. These are manageable, but they need planning. Companies that start early avoid delays.

## Moving from contractor to employee

Some businesses begin by working with freelancers in Italy. Over time the role grows. The person becomes central to the team. The company then wants a stable, clear employment relationship that protects both sides.

Converting a long-term contractor into an employee is a common step. It gives the worker security and gives the company clarity about the relationship.

## What these reasons have in common

In every case the company wants a real presence in Italy without a large upfront burden. The first hire is a bridge. It connects the company to people, customers and suppliers it cannot reach well from outside.

Before hiring, it helps to be clear on a few points:

- What the role must achieve in the first months.
- Whether the person will work from home, a shared space or a client site.
- Which collective agreement applies to the sector.
- How the company will manage and support someone in another country.

Good answers to these questions make the first hire smoother. They also make the second and third hires easier.

## The takeaway

Companies hire their first employee in Italy for talent, for customers, for proximity to partners, for speed and for low-risk testing. These reasons often overlap. What matters is having a clear goal for the role and a simple way to employ the person correctly.
