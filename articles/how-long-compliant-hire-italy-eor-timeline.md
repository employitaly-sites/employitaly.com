# How Long Does a Compliant Hire in Italy Take with an EOR?

> The path from signed offer to first payslip when you hire in Italy through an Employer of Record: the stages, what slows them down, and how to plan.

You have found the person. The offer is ready. Now you need to know when they can start and when they will be paid. This article walks through the path from offer to first payslip when you hire in Italy through an Employer of Record (EOR).

We do not give a fixed number of days here. The real timeline depends on the role, the candidate's situation and how fast each side returns documents. What we can do is show you the stages, who owns each one, and where time is usually lost.

## Stage 1: Scope the role

Everything starts with a clear description of the job. The EOR needs the job title, duties, working location, working hours, reporting line and the pay you plan to offer.

Italy sets employment terms through national collective agreements. The right agreement depends on the sector and the type of work. Choosing it correctly sets the minimum pay, notice rules, leave and contribution base. A wrong choice causes rework later.

This stage is fast when you arrive with a complete brief. It is slow when the role is still vague.

## Stage 2: Agree the terms and the contract

The EOR prepares an employment contract in line with the applicable collective agreement and Italian law. You review the commercial terms. The candidate reviews the contract.

Most delays here come from negotiation, not from paperwork. If the candidate wants changes to pay, benefits or notice, each round adds time. Settle the main points in the offer conversation before the contract is drafted.

## Stage 3: Collect candidate documents

The EOR needs identity details, tax identification and bank details. It also needs information on family circumstances that affect tax treatment.

This part sits mostly with the candidate. Ask them early to gather everything. Missing documents are the most common reason a start date slips.

## Stage 4: Right to work

If the candidate is an EU citizen, this stage is simple. If the candidate lives outside the EU, you need a work authorisation and a visa before they can start. That is a separate track with its own timing, and it often decides the whole schedule.

If you plan to hire someone from abroad, begin the visa track as soon as the offer is accepted. Do not wait for the contract to be finished. Ask your EOR to run both in parallel where possible.

If the person already lives in Italy with a valid permit, check that the permit allows this type of work.

## Stage 5: Registration with authorities

Before the start date, the EOR registers the employment with the Italian social security and labour systems. It also sets up the worker for tax withholding and contributions.

This is where an EOR earns its fee. Your company has no Italian entity, so it cannot do this itself. The EOR is the legal employer and files in its own name. Registration must be in place on time, so the start date must be fixed with enough notice.

## Stage 6: Start date and onboarding

On the first day, the employee begins work for you. The EOR gives them the required notices and a clear contact for payroll questions. You give them tools, access and a manager.

Keep a record of the actual start date and hours worked. Payroll depends on it.

## Stage 7: First payroll run

Italian payroll runs on a monthly cycle. The EOR needs your approved data before the cut-off: hours, absences, any variable pay, expenses and bonuses.

The first payslip follows the first payroll cycle after the start date. If the start date falls just after a cut-off, the first payslip comes a full cycle later. This surprises many foreign employers. Pick the start date with the payroll calendar in mind and ask your EOR for the cut-off dates.

The first run is also the one most likely to need corrections. Check the payslip together with the employee and the EOR before the next cycle.

## What slows a hire down

- A vague job brief.
- Contract negotiation that continues after drafting.
- Missing candidate documents.
- A visa track that starts late.
- A start date chosen without looking at payroll cut-offs.
- Late approval of payroll data.

## What speeds it up

- Send the full brief on the first day.
- Settle pay and notice in the offer, not in the contract.
- Ask the candidate for documents as soon as they accept.
- Run the visa and contract in parallel.
- Fix the start date after you know the payroll calendar.
- Name one person on your side who approves payroll data on time.

## A practical way to plan

Work backwards. Decide the date you want the first payslip issued. Subtract the payroll cycle. That gives you the latest start date. Subtract registration, then the contract and documents, then the visa if there is one. The result is the date by which you must send the brief.

Ask your EOR to confirm each stage in writing, with an owner and a target date. Then you can see the schedule instead of guessing it.

A compliant hire is not slow by nature. It is slow when information arrives late. Give the EOR what it needs early and the process stays predictable.
