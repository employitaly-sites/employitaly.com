# Collective agreements in Italy: why every job sits under a CCNL

> Every employee in Italy falls under a national collective agreement (CCNL). Here is what that means for pay, benefits and cost when you hire from abroad.

If you hire in Italy, you will hear the term CCNL very early. It stands for Contratto Collettivo Nazionale di Lavoro, or national collective labour agreement. It is not a side topic. It shapes almost every term of an Italian employment contract.

## What a CCNL is

A CCNL is an agreement between employer associations and trade unions for a given sector. It sets the rules that apply to everyone working in that sector, whether the employer is large, small, Italian or foreign.

The agreement covers the core terms of the job:

- Job classification and grade levels
- Minimum pay for each level
- Working hours and overtime rules
- Paid leave and sick pay
- Notice periods
- Extra pay elements and benefits
- Rules on probation and on fixed-term work

Many of these terms are not left to the employer's choice. The contract you sign with an employee refers to the CCNL, and the CCNL fills in the rest.

## Why every job ends up under one

Italy has no single national minimum wage set by statute. In practice, the CCNL fills that role. Courts use the pay levels in the relevant agreement as the benchmark for what counts as fair pay. If you pay below that benchmark, an employee can claim the difference.

So even when a CCNL is not formally binding on your company, it still applies in practice. Employers who try to avoid it usually end up with disputes, back pay claims and problems with the social security authorities.

The simple rule: every employee has a CCNL. The only real question is which one.

## Choosing the right CCNL

The CCNL is not chosen by the employee or by the foreign parent company. It follows the main activity of the employer in Italy. A software firm, a restaurant group and a manufacturing business each fall under a different agreement.

This matters for an international employer because your home-country logic may not map cleanly. A role you call "sales" or "engineering" at home may sit in a different sector in Italy, depending on what the Italian activity really is. The wrong choice can mean wrong pay levels, wrong leave rules and wrong contributions.

Several agreements can look similar on paper. They differ in pay tables, in the benefits they add and in how much flexibility they give you. Picking one is a business decision with a cost attached, and it should be made before the offer letter goes out.

## What it means for your budget

The salary you agree with a candidate is only one part of the cost. The CCNL adds to it in several ways.

**Minimum pay.** Each grade has a floor. You can pay above it, never below it.

**Extra pay elements.** Many agreements add pay components on top of the base salary. Your total annual cost can be higher than a simple monthly figure suggests.

**Benefits.** Some agreements require contributions to supplementary pension funds or health funds. These are part of the package, not optional extras.

**Leave and sickness.** The agreement defines how much paid leave staff receive and how sickness is paid. The employer often carries part of that cost.

**Termination terms.** Notice periods and the process for ending a contract depend on the grade and the agreement.

When you compare Italy to another country, compare the full cost under the right CCNL, not the headline salary.

## What it means for your HR practice

Italian employment is rules-based. The CCNL gives employees clear rights, and they know them. Payslips follow the agreement line by line. Job titles map to grades, and moving someone to a new grade has pay consequences.

This helps you in one way. The rules are known in advance, so you can plan. It limits you in another way. You cannot freely rewrite terms for one person without checking the agreement first.

A few habits help:

- Decide the grade before you decide the title.
- Keep job descriptions close to what the person really does. Grade disputes often start with a gap between the two.
- Check the agreement before promising any benefit in writing.
- Review the agreement when it is renewed, because pay tables and terms change.

## Where an Employer of Record helps

If you have no Italian entity, you still need a compliant employment relationship. An Employer of Record (EOR) hires the person in Italy on your behalf. It applies the right CCNL, issues the contract, runs payroll and handles the contributions.

You direct the daily work. The EOR carries the employer obligations under the agreement. That removes the need to learn each sector's rules yourself before you can make your first hire.

It also helps with the early decisions: which CCNL fits the role, which grade is correct, and what the real annual cost will be. Getting those right at the start is far cheaper than fixing them later.

## The short version

In Italy, the CCNL is the rulebook for the job. It sets pay floors, leave, notice and benefits. You do not opt out of it, and you should not guess which one applies. Pick the right agreement, budget for the full cost, and keep your contracts aligned with it.
