# Hiring Engineers in Turin: The Polytechnic and Automotive Ecosystem

> Turin combines a leading technical university with a deep automotive and mechatronics industry. Here is what foreign employers should know before hiring.

Turin is Italy's best-known city for mechanical and automotive engineering. If your company needs engineers in vehicle design, powertrain, embedded software, testing or industrial automation, it belongs on your shortlist.

This article explains why the talent is there, how the local ecosystem works, and what to check before you hire.

## The Polytechnic is the anchor

The Politecnico di Torino is the main source of engineering graduates in the city. It is a technical university focused on engineering and architecture. Its programmes cover mechanical, aerospace, electrical, electronic, computer and industrial engineering. Many of its graduates stay in the Turin area.

For a foreign employer, this matters in three ways.

- **A steady pipeline.** Each year new graduates enter the market with solid technical training.
- **Research links with industry.** The university works with companies on applied projects. Engineers there are used to real industrial problems, not only theory.
- **International exposure.** Many programmes are taught in English, so graduates often work comfortably with foreign teams.

## An industry that never left

Turin built its identity on the car. Large manufacturers, design studios, component makers and testing firms are all part of the local fabric. Around them sits a wide supplier base: machining, tooling, plastics, electronics, software and engineering services.

This gives you something a university alone cannot. You can hire people who have already worked on production programmes. They know homologation, quality systems and supplier management. They have seen a product go from drawing to assembly line.

The sector is also changing. Electrification, connected vehicles, driver assistance and new materials are reshaping the work. Engineers in Turin are moving into battery systems, power electronics, simulation and software-defined vehicle platforms. The skill base is broadening beyond classic mechanics.

## Beyond cars

The same talent pool serves other fields. Aerospace, rail, robotics, industrial machinery and energy equipment all draw on the same engineers. If you are a company in one of these areas, you can recruit from a larger pool than the word "automotive" suggests.

Design is another strength. Turin has a long tradition of vehicle styling and industrial design. Engineers and designers often work close together here, which helps with product development.

## Who is a good fit for a Turin hire

Turin works well for:

- Foreign automotive suppliers that need a local engineering presence near Italian manufacturers.
- Startups building hardware, battery or mobility products that need prototyping skills.
- Software and simulation companies serving the vehicle industry.
- Industrial groups that want a European engineering team without opening a full entity.

It is less obvious for companies that need a large number of people in finance or sales. Other Italian cities have deeper pools for those roles.

## Practical costs to check

Salary is only part of the cost of an employee. In Turin you should also look at local taxes that affect take-home pay and how you present offers.

The city applies a municipal income tax surcharge. According to a secondary source, the rate is split into four brackets: 0.8%, 0.8%, 1.1% and 1.2%. There is also an exemption threshold of EUR 11,790. The surcharge is paid by the employee and withheld through payroll, so it does not add to your employer cost. It does change what a candidate receives net, so it is worth including in salary discussions. Confirm the current figures before you finalise any offer, since this source is not an official one.

Other items to plan for:

- The national collective agreement that applies to your sector. Engineering and metalworking roles usually fall under a specific agreement that sets minimum pay, notice and leave.
- Employer social contributions, which sit on top of gross salary.
- Payroll, benefits and reporting, all handled in Italian.

## Hiring without your own Italian company

Many foreign firms want to hire two or three engineers in Turin before deciding on a legal entity. Setting up a subsidiary takes time and requires local administration, accounting and payroll from day one.

An Employer of Record (EOR) is the faster route. The EOR becomes the legal employer in Italy. It issues the contract, runs payroll, pays contributions and handles compliance. You manage the daily work and the engineer's tasks. You keep control of the project, and the EOR handles the paperwork.

This suits teams that want to test the market, hire quickly, or keep headcount small. If you later open your own entity, the employees can be transferred.

An EOR can also help when the candidate is not from the European Union. Visa and relocation support matter in a city with a strong international student and graduate community.

## How to start

1. Define the role and the skills you need: CAD, simulation, embedded systems, testing or process engineering.
2. Check which collective agreement fits the job.
3. Decide where the person will work: your own space, a coworking site or from home.
4. Agree on the contract type and salary, including the net impact of local taxes.
5. Choose whether to hire through an EOR or through your own entity.

Turin gives you engineers with real industrial experience and a university that keeps producing new ones. For companies in automotive and related fields, that combination is hard to match elsewhere in Italy.
