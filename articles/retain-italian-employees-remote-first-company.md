# How to Retain Italian Employees in a Remote-First Company

> Belonging and growth keep Italian staff in a remote-first company. Practical steps for foreign employers hiring in Italy: contact, careers, recognition.

You hired well in Italy. Now you want people to stay. In a remote-first company, that depends on two things: whether they feel they belong, and whether they can see a future.

Pay matters. But pay alone rarely keeps a good person. Belonging and growth do more work than most managers expect.

## Why remote makes belonging harder

An Italian employee working from home often sits far from your head office. They may be the only person from their country on the team. They miss the hallway talk. They miss the small signals that tell you whether you are valued.

In many Italian workplaces, relationships carry weight. People like to know who they work with. They want to be seen as a person, not a task list. A remote setup that treats them as a resource will feel cold fast.

The risk is quiet. People rarely complain. They update their profile and take a call from a recruiter.

## Build belonging on purpose

Do not leave it to chance. Small, steady habits work better than a big yearly event.

- **Hold regular video calls with cameras on.** Keep them short. Make room for a few words about life before the agenda.
- **Meet in person when you can.** Bring the Italian team to your main office, or send leaders to Italy. Face time builds trust that lasts for months.
- **Pair new hires with a buddy.** Pick someone who is not their manager. Give them time to answer simple questions.
- **Share context, not only tasks.** Explain why the company makes its choices. People stay where they understand the direction.
- **Respect local rhythm.** Know the national holidays. Do not schedule key calls at hours that ignore local habits. Small signs of respect add up.

## Make the employment feel solid

Italian employees care about security. A clear contract, correct payslips and on-time payments tell them you are serious. If something goes wrong with payroll or paperwork, trust drops quickly and is hard to rebuild.

This is where an Employer of Record (EOR) helps. The EOR is the legal employer in Italy. It handles contracts, payroll, contributions and compliance. Your team in Italy gets a proper local employment relationship. You keep day-to-day control of the work. Fewer administrative errors means fewer reasons for doubt.

## Growth: show a path

Remote workers worry about being forgotten. Out of sight can mean out of mind when promotions are discussed. Fight this directly.

**Write down career steps.** Define what the next level looks like. Say what skills and results it takes. Do it for roles held in Italy, not only at headquarters.

**Hold real career talks.** Not just performance reviews. Ask what the person wants to learn and where they want to be in a few years. Then act on the answer.

**Fund learning.** Give a budget and time for courses, books and conferences. Let people choose. Italian professionals often value training in their field and in languages.

**Offer stretch work.** Let people lead a project, mentor a newcomer or present to leadership. Visibility counts when you are remote.

**Promote from within.** When a role opens, look at your Italian team first. Each internal move shows the others that growth is real.

## Recognition without theatre

Praise should be specific and timely. Say what the person did and why it mattered. Do it in public when that fits, and in private when it does not.

Avoid empty perks. A gift card does less than a manager who understands the work. Avoid slogans about being a family. Italian employees tend to prefer honest, respectful treatment over corporate warmth.

## Listen and act

Ask for feedback often. Short surveys help, but a direct conversation is better. When you hear a problem, fix it and report back. People stop talking when nothing changes.

Watch for warning signs:

- Cameras that stay off and silence in meetings.
- Fewer questions and less initiative.
- Declining requests to join optional sessions.
- A manager who has not spoken to the person one to one in a long time.

Act early. A frank conversation costs little. Replacing a trained employee costs far more.

## Managers make the difference

People leave managers more than companies. Train your leaders to run remote teams well. They should give clear goals, check in without hovering, and trust people to deliver. Make sure managers outside Italy understand local expectations on working time, leave and communication.

## A short plan

- Set a rhythm of team calls and one to one talks.
- Plan in-person time for the Italian team.
- Write career paths and share them.
- Fund learning and let people pick.
- Keep payroll and contracts accurate through a reliable local employer.
- Ask for feedback and show what you changed.

Retention is not a perk program. It is a habit of showing people they matter and that they can grow with you. Do that consistently, and your Italian team will have good reasons to stay.
