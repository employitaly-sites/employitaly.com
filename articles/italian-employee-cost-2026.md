# What an Italian employee really costs in 2026

> Gross salary is only the start. See how employer pension contributions, workplace insurance and accruals build the real cost of an employee in Italy.

If you plan to hire in Italy, the gross salary in the contract is not your cost. The real cost is higher. It is the gross salary plus employer charges plus amounts you set aside during the year. This article explains each layer so you can budget with fewer surprises.

## Start with gross salary

Gross salary is the amount before the employee's own contributions and income tax. It is the base for most of what follows. Your national collective agreement sets minimum pay levels and many extra rules. In Italy, salary is often paid in more monthly instalments than the calendar suggests. Budget for the full annual amount, not just twelve equal payments.

When you compare Italy with another market, compare total annual gross, not the monthly figure.

## Employer social security contributions

The employer pays social security contributions on top of the gross salary. They are calculated on the taxable base, which is the pay subject to contributions.

The largest block is the pension scheme for old age, invalidity and survivors, known as IVS. For the employer, the reported rate is 23.81% of the taxable base. This comes from a secondary source, so treat it as a planning figure and check it against the current INPS circular before you sign an offer.

IVS is not the only contribution. Other employer contributions can apply, depending on company size, sector and the collective agreement. They cover areas such as sickness, maternity, unemployment support and wage guarantee funds. Rates and rules differ by case. A single flat percentage for "Italian employer charges" is not reliable. Ask for a calculation based on the actual role and sector.

## Workplace insurance

Employers also pay a premium to INAIL, the national workplace accident insurance body. The premium depends on the risk class of the work. An office role and a warehouse role do not cost the same.

The rules are changing. The 2019 tariffs, set by the interministerial decree of 27 February 2019, remain in force. The way they are applied was modified by a ministerial decree dated 2 April 2026 and published on 3 June 2026. So the tariff table is the same, but the method used to apply it has changed. If you use a budget template from last year, update the INAIL line. Your payroll provider should confirm the premium for each position.

## Accruals you must plan for

Some costs do not appear as a monthly payment. They build up and become payable later. This is where many foreign employers underestimate the budget.

- **Severance accrual (TFR).** Each year, a share of pay is set aside for the employee. It is paid when the employment ends. You must book it as a liability from the first month.
- **Additional monthly salary payments.** Many collective agreements require extra payments during the year. If you divide the annual figure into twelve parts, you will underbudget each month.
- **Paid leave and holidays.** Untaken leave builds up and has a cost. Sick pay and other paid absences add to it.
- **Notice and exit costs.** Notice periods follow the collective agreement and depend on seniority. Plan for them in your financial forecast.

These items also attract contributions in many cases. Do not count them separately and forget the charges on top.

## How to build the real figure

Use this order when you prepare a cost estimate for a role:

- Fix the total annual gross salary, including all contractual payments.
- Add employer contributions on the taxable base, including IVS and the others that apply to your sector and size.
- Add the INAIL premium for the risk class of the role.
- Add the severance accrual and other accruals.
- Add the cost of benefits required by the collective agreement.
- Add any local costs, such as equipment, training and payroll processing.

The result is the true annual cost of the employee. Divide it by the gross salary to get your own multiplier. Use that multiplier for budgeting across roles, but recalculate it when the sector or the collective agreement changes.

## Common mistakes

**Using the wrong collective agreement.** It drives pay levels, extra payments, notice and leave. Choosing it late changes every number.

**Using one rate for every role.** Risk class and company size change the charges.

**Forgetting accruals.** The severance liability is real from day one, even if you pay it years later.

**Relying on old tables.** The INAIL application method changed in 2026. Contribution rules are also updated from time to time.
