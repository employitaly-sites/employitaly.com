# Using universities and alumni networks to build your Italian brand

> How foreign companies can use Italian universities and alumni networks to become a known employer in Italy before they hire, and what to do first.

A foreign company that wants to hire in Italy has a quiet problem. Almost nobody there knows its name. Strong candidates choose employers they have heard of. Universities and alumni networks are one of the cheapest ways to fix that.

This article explains how campus and alumni channels work for a company with no Italian presence yet.

## Why campuses matter

Italian universities have close ties to the local economy. Professors advise firms. Career offices place graduates. Students talk to each other about who is worth applying to.

A company that shows up early becomes familiar. When it later posts a role, people recognise it. That recognition lowers the effort of every hire that follows.

This works for experienced roles too. Many senior professionals keep in touch with their department and read its newsletters.

## Start with the career office

Most universities run a careers or placement office. It is the front door.

Contact it with a clear message. Say who you are, what you do, and what kind of people you want to meet. Do not send a vague note about partnership. Offer something concrete.

Useful offers include:

- A guest talk on a real problem your team solves.
- A short case study students can work on.
- A named contact who answers questions from students.
- An open day, online or in person, about working in your field.

Keep the offer small and easy to say yes to. A talk costs you an hour. It can earn you months of goodwill.

## Work with departments, not only central offices

Central offices are busy. A single department is closer to the subject you care about.

If you hire engineers, talk to the engineering faculty. If you hire finance staff, talk to economics. A professor who knows your work may point strong students your way without being asked.

Offer to share real data or tools with a course. Offer to review a thesis topic. These gestures show you take the place seriously.

## Use alumni networks

Alumni associations are often more useful than the university itself. They hold lists of graduates, run events, and publish updates. Many graduates work abroad or in international firms, and they like to help people from their own school.

Find the alumni groups tied to the schools you target. Ask to sponsor an event or share a job note in their newsletter. Ask whether any members work in your sector and would speak at a session.

Also look at your own side. If your team includes people who studied in Italy, ask them to introduce you. A personal message from an alumnus carries more weight than any brand page.

## Be present on the student side

Student groups organise events, competitions and clubs. Many of them welcome company support in the form of mentors, judges or prizes.

Pick a few and stay with them. Showing up once looks like marketing. Showing up every year looks like commitment.

## Keep your message honest and local

Present your company in Italian where it counts. Talks can be in English if your working language is English. State this clearly so no one is surprised.

Be direct about what you offer. Say what the job is, where the person will work, and what the career path looks like. Italian graduates are well aware of the local market. They notice vague promises.

If you cannot yet employ people directly in Italy, say how you will. Many foreign firms use an Employer of Record (EOR) to hire locally before they open their own entity. Explain this plainly. Candidates want to know who their employer is, how they are paid, and who handles their contract.

## Prepare the hiring side first

Campus work creates interest. You need to be ready to act on it.

Before you approach a university, check that you can:

- Issue a proper Italian employment contract.
- Pay people on time, in line with local rules.
- Answer questions about benefits and working hours.
- Reply to applicants within days, not weeks.

If these are missing, interest turns into frustration. A slow response damages the name you just built.

## Measure what you can

Track simple things. How many students attended a talk. How many applications mention the event. Which schools send the best candidates. Use this to decide where to spend time next.

Do not expect fast results. Reputation builds over seasons, not weeks.

## A practical way to begin

Pick a small number of universities that match your sector. Contact the career office and one department at each. Join their alumni networks. Offer a talk or a case study. Follow up with every contact.

Then repeat the next year. The brand grows from that rhythm.
