# Employer Brand Checklist for an Unknown Foreign Company in Italy

> A practical checklist of credibility signals that help a foreign company nobody knows in Italy win trust from candidates, before the first hire.

Italian candidates have never heard of your company. That is normal. It also means they will check you before they answer your message. They will look at your website, your LinkedIn page, your job post and how you write back. If these look thin, the candidate assumes risk and moves on.

This checklist covers the signals that make an unknown foreign employer look real. None of it costs much. Most of it is about being clear.

## Show who is behind the company

Candidates want to know that real people stand behind the offer. A logo and a careers page are not enough.

- Name the founders or leaders, with photos and honest bios.
- Link to their personal professional profiles.
- State where the company is registered and what it does, in plain words.
- Give a way to reach a human: a named contact, not only a form.

If the hiring manager is visible and answers questions, the company stops being an anonymous foreign name.

## Explain how the employment works in Italy

The biggest fear is practical. Who is my employer? Who pays me? Where do my contributions go? A foreign company without an Italian entity can feel risky to an Italian professional.

Say it early and clearly:

- Who the legal employer is in Italy.
- That the contract follows Italian rules and the applicable national collective agreement.
- That payroll and social contributions are handled locally.
- Who the candidate calls if something goes wrong with pay or paperwork.

An Employer of Record (EOR) model answers these points directly. The candidate gets an Italian contract and local administration. You keep day-to-day management. Say this on the job page, not only at offer stage.

## Write job posts like an Italian employer would

A post translated word by word from another market looks careless. Review it for these points.

- Write in good Italian, or good English if the role is truly international. Have a native speaker read it.
- Use the job titles Italian professionals actually search for.
- State the contract type, work location or remote arrangement, and the reporting line.
- Give a salary range. Italian candidates notice when it is missing.
- Describe the real tasks, not a list of buzzwords.

A clear post tells the candidate you understand the local market.

## Be transparent about pay and benefits

Compensation in Italy is read as gross annual pay, with benefits and any additional monthly payments described separately. Foreign companies often quote a single figure with no breakdown. That raises suspicion.

Show the structure. Say what is fixed and what is variable. Say what the company adds, such as equipment, training or flexible hours. If you do not know the local norms, ask your EOR partner to check your offer before you publish it.

## Make the company findable

Before replying, a candidate will search your name. Check what they find.

- A professional website with an About page and a clear product or service description.
- A complete company profile on the main professional network, with current staff listed.
- Reviews or employee comments, even if few. Silence is read as a warning.
- A press mention, customer list or case study that proves the business exists.

If you have no Italian presence, say why you are hiring there. A short, honest reason works better than vague ambition.

## Treat the process as part of the brand

How you run hiring tells candidates how you will run employment.

- Reply quickly and say what happens next.
- Keep interviews on time and use a named interviewer.
- Tell candidates the full process up front.
- Send the written offer in a format they can show a lawyer, a union contact or family.
- Never ask for sensitive documents before an offer is made.

Candidates talk to each other. A careless process travels faster than any careers page.

## Prepare for the questions you will get

Expect candidates to ask about stability. Will the company still be here next year? Is the Italian role a test? Prepare honest answers. If the role is new and the team is small, say so, and explain the plan. Candidates accept uncertainty more easily than evasion.

Also be ready for questions about notice, leave, sick pay and termination. You do not need to recite the law. You need a partner who can answer correctly and quickly.

## A short self-check

Before you publish your first Italian job post, ask yourself:

- Can a stranger tell who runs the company within a minute?
- Is the legal employer named?
- Does the post include pay, contract type and location?
- Is the language natural to a local reader?
- Would you accept the offer if you were the candidate?

If any answer is no, fix it first. Credibility is not built by claims. It is built by removing reasons to doubt you.
