# High earners in Italy: the contribution ceiling and the extra 1% explained

> How the INPS contribution ceiling and the extra 1% above the threshold affect executive packages in Italy, with the figures employers need for budgeting.

If you plan to hire a senior manager or executive in Italy, social contributions will not grow in a straight line with salary. Two rules change the picture for high earners: an extra 1% above a threshold, and a ceiling on the earnings that count. This FAQ explains both and what they mean for your package design.

## What is the extra 1% contribution?

Italian employees pay pension contributions to INPS. For pay above a set threshold, an additional 1% applies on top of the ordinary rate. It applies only to the portion above the threshold, not to the whole salary.

The threshold is EUR 56,224 per year. On a monthly basis, that is EUR 4,685.

An executive earning well above this level will therefore pay the extra 1% on every euro above the line. The effect on a single payslip is small. On a large package it adds up.

## What is the contribution ceiling?

The ceiling, called the *massimale contributivo*, is a cap on the annual earnings that count for pension contributions. For employees enrolled with INPS from 1996 with no earlier contribution history, the ceiling is EUR 122,295 per year.

Earnings above that amount do not generate further pension contributions. That matters most for senior hires, who often sit well above it.

## Does the ceiling apply to everyone?

No. The figure above is for workers who started contributing from 1996 with no earlier seniority. People with earlier contribution history can fall under different rules. Many international executives arrive with a mixed record, so check the specific case before you fix the budget.

## How do these rules affect an executive package?

Think of the package in bands.

**Below the threshold.** Ordinary contributions only. This band is simple to model.

**Between the threshold and the ceiling.** Ordinary contributions plus the extra 1% on the part above EUR 56,224. Each additional euro of gross pay costs slightly more in contributions than the one before it.

**Above the ceiling.** Pension contributions stop growing on the part above EUR 122,295 per year. Bonus and variable pay in this band do not add to the pension contribution base in the same way as pay in the lower bands.

For an employer, this means the cost of a raise or a bonus depends on where the executive's pay already sits. A bonus paid to someone already above the ceiling behaves differently from the same bonus paid to someone below it.

## What should I do when I split fixed and variable pay?

Split your package and test each part against the bands above. A few practical points:

- Model the full year, not only the monthly payslip. The ceiling is annual, so contributions can change late in the year as the cap is reached.
- Place bonuses in the calendar year where you want them to count. Timing can move a payment from one side of the ceiling to the other.
- Run the numbers for the net pay your executive expects. Gross-to-net in Italy depends on contributions and on income tax, and you need both.
- Keep the base salary realistic. Contract terms, notice and severance in Italy are tied to pay levels, so a low base with a large bonus has consequences beyond contributions.

## Does the ceiling make a senior hire cheaper?

It caps one cost line, but it is not a discount. Income tax still applies to the full pay. Other employer costs and contract obligations remain. The ceiling limits pension contributions on the top slice of earnings. It does not change the rest of the cost of employment.

## How reliable are these figures?

The figures here come from a secondary source that explains the INPS rules, not from an INPS circular read directly. Thresholds and ceilings are updated by INPS from time to time. Confirm the current values against INPS before you sign an offer or set a budget.

## What does this mean for foreign employers?

A company without an Italian entity cannot easily run Italian payroll and contributions on its own. An Employer of Record (EOR) hires the person in Italy, runs payroll, applies the thresholds and the ceiling correctly, and files with INPS. You keep day-to-day management of the executive. The EOR carries the compliance work and gives you a cost model you can check before the offer goes out.
